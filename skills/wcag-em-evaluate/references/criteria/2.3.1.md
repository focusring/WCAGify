# 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold

Level A · WCAG 2.0+ · Guideline 2.3 Seizures and Physical Reactions · Group: `motion`

Sources:
[Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)
· [Spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#three-flashes-or-below-threshold)
· [Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/#three-flashes-or-below-threshold)
· [Plain English](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/2-3-1-three-flashes-or-below-threshold/)
· ACT: none mapped to this criterion.

## Requirement

> Web pages do not contain anything that flashes more than three times in any one second period,
> or the flash is below the general flash and red flash thresholds.
>
> Note:
> Since any content that does not meet this success criterion can interfere with a user's
> ability to use the whole page, all content on the web page (whether it is used to
> meet other success criteria or not) must meet this success criterion. See Conformance
> Requirement 5: Non-Interference.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary):

**flash**: "a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance that can cause seizures in some people
if it is large enough and in the right frequency range". The closing clause carries the test: a
change that is small enough or slow enough is not a flash at all.

**general flash and red flash thresholds**: "a flash or rapidly changing image sequence is below
the threshold (i.e., content passes) if any of the following are true: there are no more than three
general flashes and / or no more than three red flashes within any one-second period; or the
combined area of flashes occurring concurrently occupies no more than a total of .006 steradians
within any 10 degree visual field on the screen (25% of any 10 degree visual field on the screen)
at typical viewing distance where: A general flash is defined as a pair of opposing changes in
relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum relative luminance (1.0) where the relative
luminance of the darker image is below 0.80; and where 'a pair of opposing changes' is an increase
followed by a decrease, or a decrease followed by an increase, and A red flash is defined as any
pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated red". Either bullet on its own is enough to
pass; a general flash needs both the 10 % swing _and_ the darker state under 0.80.

Its _Exception_: "Flashing that is a fine, balanced, pattern such as white noise or an alternating
checkerboard pattern with 'squares' smaller than 0.1 degree (of visual field at typical viewing
distance) on a side does not violate the thresholds."

Its Note 1: "using a 341 x 256 pixel rectangle anywhere on the displayed screen area when the
content is viewed at 1024 x 768 pixels will provide a good estimate of a 10 degree visual field".
The Understanding substitutes CSS pixels for those reference pixels, so the 10 degree field is
341 × 256 CSS px = 87,296 CSS px², and 25 % of it is 21,824 CSS px² — the "small safe area" that
[G176](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G176) names in Formula 1.

Its Note 2: "A transition is the change in relative luminance (or relative luminance/color for red
flashing) between adjacent peaks and valleys in a plot of relative luminance [...] measurement
against time. A flash consists of two opposing transitions." Count transitions, then halve: the
count is the measurement, the flash figure is derived from it.

Its Note 3 (red, new in WCAG 2.2): a "pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated red" is "a
pair of opposing transitions where, one transition is either to or from a state with a value
R/(R + G + B) that is greater than or equal to 0.8, and the difference between states is more than
0.2 (unitless) in the CIE 1976 UCS chromaticity diagram. [ISO_9241-391]". Both halves must hold: a
saturated-red endpoint with a chromaticity difference of 0.2 or less is not a red flash, and a big
chromaticity swing between two non-red states is not one either.

Its Note 4: "no tool is necessary to evaluate for this condition if flashing is less than or equal
to 3 flashes in any one second. Content automatically passes". A measured 3 or fewer closes the
criterion; only a measured 4 or more sends the source to a tool.

**blinking** (Understanding key term): "switch back and forth between two visual states in a way
that is meant to draw attention", with the note "It is possible for something to be large enough
and blink brightly enough at the right frequency to be also classified as a flash". W3C draws the
line explicitly: "Blinking usually does not occur at speeds of 3 per second or more, but it can. If
blinking occurs faster than 3 per second, it would also be considered a flash." Slower blink is
2.2.2 only; faster blink is this criterion as well.

**CSS pixel**: "visual angle of about 0.0213 degrees". The exception's 0.1 degree square is 3.4 CSS
px wide by the 341-per-10-degrees conversion and 4.7 CSS px by this one; treat a pattern whose
squares are 5 CSS px or more as outside the exception.

**web page**: "a non-embedded resource obtained from a single URI using HTTP plus any other
resources that are used in the rendering or intended to be rendered together with it by a user
agent". W3C's own example is an AJAX mail program where "Links or buttons are provided that cause
the inbox, contacts, or calendar to display, but do not change the URI of the page as a whole" —
every such state is the same web page, so every state is inside this criterion.

**relative luminance**: "L = 0.2126 * R + 0.7152 * G + 0.0722 * B" over sRGB channels linearised
with "if RsRGB <= 0.04045 then R = RsRGB/12.92 else R = ((RsRGB+0.055)/1.055) ^ 2.4". The 10 % swing
and the 0.80 ceiling are on that 0–1 scale, never on 8-bit values.

## In plain terms

Nothing on the page may flash more than three times in any second: no video, animated image,
blinking element or third-party embed, decorative or not. W3C's goal line is "Content does not
trigger seizures", because flashing content "can cause migraines, dizziness, nausea, and seizures".
Faster flashing passes only below the thresholds: one flashing area under 21,824 CSS px², or a
luminance swing under 10 %, or a darker state at 0.80 or above, and no saturated red. A seizure
"could occur faster than most users could turn it off", so a pause control, a warning or a
reduced-motion rule is no defence. Only flicker "caused by the content itself" is judged: flicker
produced by the display or by the computer rendering the image is outside the author's control.

## Applies when

- Present: content whose luminance or colour goes back and forth, third-party embeds included:
  `<video>` and embedded players (`media.js` `media[]`, `kind` 'video' or 'embed'); animated GIF,
  APNG or WebP (`media.js` `animatedImages[]`); repeating CSS or Web animations (`motion.js`
  `animations[]`, `playState` 'running', `iterations` 'infinite' or above 1); `<blink>` (`motion.js`
  `blink`); script toggling of class, style or `src` (`motion.js` `autoUpdating[]`, `changesIn5s` 6
  or more: three flashes are six opposing changes); `<canvas>` and WebGL (`get count "canvas"`);
  auto-advancing carousels (`motion.js` `carousels[]`, `autoAdvances` true). Intent names the
  typical carriers: "a video clip or animated image of a series of strobe flashes, or close-ups of
  rapid-fire explosions". Non-Interference: check every state; content exempt elsewhere counts here.
- Blinking that is fast enough: a blink above three times a second is a flash here as well as
  2.2.2 content, so measure it instead of filing it only under 2.2.2.
- Not present: no `media[]` entry of kind 'video' or 'embed', no `animatedImages[]`, no `<canvas>`,
  no running repeating animation, no `autoUpdating[]` entry with 6 or more changes, in every state.
- Exceptions: the content passes when any holds: (1) at most three general and three red flashes in
  any one-second window ([G19](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G19)); (2) the
  contiguous area flashing at once stays within 25 % of any 10 degree field (341 × 256 CSS px), so
  at most 21,824 CSS px² ([G176](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G176)); (3) a
  luminance swing under 10 % or a darker state at or above 0.80, with no saturated red: not a flash;
  (4) a fine, balanced pattern: white noise, or a checkerboard whose squares are under 0.1 degree.
- Exception (2) has a second condition. G176's own test is "Check that only one area of the screen
  is flashing at any time" and "Check that the flashing content would fit into a contiguous
  container whose area is less than the small safe area", with "Expected Results: Checks #2 and #3
  are true". Two separated areas flashing concurrently take the exception away, whatever each one
  measures.
- Scale: "Content should be analyzed at the largest scale at which a user may view the content, and
  at the standard zoom level of the user agent. For example, with a video that may play in an area
  of a web page and also at full screen, the video should be analyzed for risks at full screen." So
  a full-screen-capable source is judged on the viewport area, not on its inline box; and do not
  re-measure at 200 % zoom, standard zoom is the reference.
- Looping: "For short clips that might be looped (such as GIF animations), the content should be
  analyzed while looping" — measure across the loop seam, not a single pass.
- Beyond sRGB: for video "provided in color spaces other than sRGB, the version provided with the
  highest dynamic range should be tested", where a general flash is "a change in luminance of 20
  cd/m2 or more where the darker image is below 160 cd/m2. (ITU-R BT.1702.)", and for HDR with the
  darker state at 160 cd/m2 or more "a general flash is one where the Michelson contrast is 1/17 or
  greater". A canvas read returns sRGB only: record `cannot-tell` and send the source to a tool.
- Viewing distance is not a fail reason: W3C notes "Users that are closer to their screens than the
  idealized viewing distance will be affected by flashing areas that normatively pass. The same
  problem applies to users who rely on zoom or screen magnification." A normative pass stays a pass;
  put the concern in the recommendation.

## axe-core

No axe-core rule is tagged with this criterion and no ACT rule is mapped to it, so `agent-browser
a11y` returns neither a violation nor an incomplete for 2.3.1 on any sample. Everything below is
manual, and the axe run never settles this criterion in either direction.

## Manual procedure

Measure at `set viewport 1280 900` in the default media mode: no reduced-motion or zoom exception
exists, and W3C fixes the analysis at "the standard zoom level of the user agent". Follow W3C's own
branch order ([G15](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G15)): "If material flashes 3
times per second or less then the simple test in G19 can be used. If material flashes in only one
place on screen at a time and is quite small then the simple test in technique G176 can be used.
For all other types, a tool is needed."

1. Inventory the candidates:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/motion.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/media.js
   agent-browser get count "canvas"
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   ```

   Keep every `media[]` and `animatedImages[]` item; every running `animations[]` entry with
   `iterations` 'infinite' or above 1 (a `duration` under 334 ms can exceed three flashes per
   second, multi-flip keyframes at longer durations too); `autoUpdating[]` entries with
   `changesIn5s` 6 or more; every `carousels[]` entry with `autoAdvances` true; every `<canvas>`.
   Record `reducedMotionQueryUsed` and `marquee` as context only. Neither probe counts flashes.
   Done when each candidate is in the findings file with kind and selector, or the list is empty.

2. Measure one candidate per run: put its selector in `sels`, set `ms` (5000 for an element, 10000
   for a `<video>`); an `eval` that runs longer than 25 s never returns. The script counts as the
   glossary does: peaks and valleys of relative luminance, general transitions of 10 % or more with
   the darker state under 0.80, red transitions where one state has R/(R + G + B) at or above 0.8
   _and_ the CIE 1976 UCS distance between the states is more than 0.2, worst one-second window:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (async () => {
     const sels = ['#hero-video'], ms = 5000 // one candidate per step-2 run; 10000 for a <video>
     const chan = (v) => ((v /= 255) <= 0.04045 ? v / 12.92 : ((v + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4)
     const lum = (R, G, B) => 0.2126 * R + 0.7152 * G + 0.0722 * B
     const rgb = (c) => ((c || '').match(/[\d.]+/g) || [0, 0, 0, 0]).map(Number)
     const tick = () => new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 5)) // rAF runs at 1 fps here: do not use
     const uv = (R, G, B) => { // CIE 1976 UCS, per the Understanding's red-flash note
       const X = 0.4124564 * R + 0.3575761 * G + 0.1804375 * B
       const Y = 0.2126729 * R + 0.7151522 * G + 0.0721750 * B
       const Z = 0.0193339 * R + 0.1191920 * G + 0.9503041 * B, d = X + 15 * Y + 3 * Z
       return d ? [(4 * X) / d, (9 * Y) / d] : [0, 0] }
     const pt = (t, R, G, B) => { const s = R + G + B, [u, v] = uv(R, G, B)
       return { t, L: lum(R, G, B), r: s ? R / s : 0, u, v } }
     const perSec = (T) => // half-open window: transitions 1000 ms apart are one second's worth
       Math.max(0, ...T.map((a) => T.filter((b) => b >= a && b - a < 1000).length))
     const minGap = (T) =>
       T.length < 2 ? null : Math.min(...T.slice(1).map((t, i) => t - T[i]))
     const extrema = (pts, k, h) => { if (pts.length < 2) return [] // 0.05 hysteresis
       const ext = [pts[0]]; let dir = 0, cand = pts[0]
       for (const p of pts.slice(1)) { const d = p[k] - cand[k]
         if (!dir) { if (Math.abs(d) >= h) { dir = Math.sign(d); cand = p } }
         else if (Math.sign(d) === dir) cand = p
         else if (Math.abs(d) >= h) { ext.push(cand); dir = -dir; cand = p } }
       ext.push(cand); return ext }
     const generalT = (pts) => { const e = extrema(pts, 'L', 0.05)
       return e.slice(1).filter((p, i) => Math.abs(p.L - e[i].L) >= 0.1 &&
         Math.min(p.L, e[i].L) < 0.8).map((p) => Math.round(p.t)) }
     const redT = (pts) => { const e = extrema(pts, 'r', 0.05)
       return e.slice(1).filter((p, i) => (p.r >= 0.8 || e[i].r >= 0.8) &&
         Math.hypot(p.u - e[i].u, p.v - e[i].v) > 0.2).map((p) => Math.round(p.t)) }
     const canvas = Object.assign(document.createElement('canvas'), { width: 32, height: 24 })
     const ctx = canvas.getContext('2d', { willReadFrequently: true }), out = []
     const frame = (src, t) => { ctx.drawImage(src, 0, 0, 32, 24); let R = 0, G = 0, B = 0
       const d = ctx.getImageData(0, 0, 32, 24).data
       for (let i = 0; i < d.length; i += 4) {
         R += chan(d[i]); G += chan(d[i + 1]); B += chan(d[i + 2]) }
       return pt(t, R / 768, G / 768, B / 768) }
     for (const sel of sels) {
       const el = document.querySelector(sel)
       if (!el) { out.push({ sel, missing: true }); continue }
       if (/^(img|canvas|svg|iframe|embed|object)$/.test(el.localName)) {
         out.push({ sel, kind: el.localName, error: 'step 4' }); continue }
       const kind = el.localName === 'video' ? 'video' : 'element'
       const box = el.getBoundingClientRect(), t0 = performance.now(), pts = []
       try {
         if (kind === 'video') {
           el.muted = true; el.currentTime = 0; await el.play()
           while (performance.now() - t0 < ms && !el.ended) {
             await tick(); pts.push(frame(el, performance.now() - t0)) }
           el.pause()
         } else {
           const pc = rgb(getComputedStyle(el.parentElement).backgroundColor)
           const back = (pc[3] ?? 1) >= 1 ? pc : [255, 255, 255]
           while (performance.now() - t0 < ms) {
             await tick()
             const cs = getComputedStyle(el), c = rgb(cs.backgroundColor)
             const shown = cs.visibility !== 'hidden' && +cs.opacity >= 0.5 && (c[3] ?? 1) >= 0.5
             const [r8, g8, b8] = shown ? c : back
             pts.push(pt(performance.now() - t0, chan(r8), chan(g8), chan(b8))) }
         }
       } catch (e) { out.push({ sel, kind, error: String(e) }); continue }
       const g = generalT(pts), rr = redT(pts), gt = perSec(g), rt = perSec(rr)
       out.push({ sel, kind, frames: pts.length, areaPx: Math.round(box.width * box.height),
         viewportPx: innerWidth * innerHeight,
         secs: +((performance.now() - t0) / 1000).toFixed(1),
         generalTransitionsPerSecond: gt, generalFlashesPerSecond: gt / 2,
         redTransitionsPerSecond: rt, redFlashesPerSecond: rt / 2,
         minGapMs: minGap(g), minRedGapMs: minGap(rr),
         maxRed: +Math.max(0, ...pts.map((p) => p.r)).toFixed(2),
         levels: [...new Set(pts.map((p) => p.L.toFixed(2)))].sort() })
     }
     return out
   })()
   JS
   ```

   Read per candidate: `generalTransitionsPerSecond` and `redTransitionsPerSecond` (the
   measurements), `generalFlashesPerSecond` and `redFlashesPerSecond` (halved, the figures to
   report), `minGapMs` and `minRedGapMs` (the shortest interval between two transitions), `areaPx`
   (rendered CSS px²) against `viewportPx`, `maxRed`, `levels` (the states seen), `frames` over
   `secs` (about 150 per second; under 40 undercounts fast flashes) and `error`
   (pixels unreadable, a cross-origin video: step 4). The transition count answers both of G19's
   checks at once — "Check that there are no more than three flashes during any 1-second period"
   and "If there are three flashes, check that the Light/Dark status at the end of the 1-second
   period is the same as at the start" — because an odd count is exactly a window that ends in the
   other state. W3C's worked case: "if there are seven transitions from dark to light or light to
   dark, it would be 3.5 flashes, which is more than the allowed three flashes (six transitions)".
   So DARK-LIGHT-DARK-LIGHT-DARK-LIGHT-DARK inside one second is 6 and passes;
   DARK-LIGHT-DARK-LIGHT-DARK-LIGHT-DARK-LIGHT is 7 and fails. Read a measured 7 as the boundary,
   not as a verdict: sampling every 5 ms places one transition up to a sample early, so content
   flashing a clean three times a second (167 ms between transitions) also reads 7. Compare
   `minGapMs` with 167 ms (three flashes a second) and 143 ms (3.5); 8 or more transitions is at
   least four flashes a second and needs no such reading. An element whose change is not its
   background colour or opacity (a background image, a filter, a flashing child) measures 0: send it
   to step 4. A video frame counts by its mean luminance, so a video with strobe-like scenes and a
   count of 6 or less goes to step 4 too. For a `<video>` past 10 s, rerun with `el.currentTime` at
   the next window. Done when each candidate has a measurement, an `error` or a step 4 note.

3. Decide the scale and whether the small-area exception is even available:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => {
     const el = document.querySelector('#hero-video'), b = el.getBoundingClientRect()
     return { areaPx: Math.round(b.width * b.height), viewportPx: innerWidth * innerHeight,
       fullscreen: el.localName === 'iframe'
         ? el.allowFullscreen || /fullscreen/.test(el.allow || '')
         : !!(el.controls && document.fullscreenEnabled) }
   })()
   JS
   ```

   With `fullscreen` true, the area to judge is `viewportPx`, not `areaPx`, and the small-area
   exception is gone for any viewport above 21,824 CSS px². Note every other source flashing at the
   same moment: with two or more, G176's "only one area of the screen is flashing at any time" is
   already false. Done when each candidate carries the area it is judged on and a yes/no on G176.

4. Sources the script cannot read (cross-origin video, embedded players, animated images,
   `<canvas>`, WebGL, SVG). Start the content (embed: `frame @e12` with its ref, `find role button
click --name "Play"`, `frame main`), then record it and take two captures:

   ```bash
   agent-browser scrollintoview "<css>"
   agent-browser record start .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>-flash-<slug>.webm
   agent-browser wait 10000
   agent-browser record stop
   agent-browser screenshot "<css>" .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>-flash-<slug>-1.png
   agent-browser screenshot "<css>" .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>-flash-<slug>-2.png
   ```

   Record at least two full cycles of a short looping clip, because W3C analyses a GIF "while
   looping": a clip that ends bright and restarts dark adds a transition at the seam. The recording
   runs at 30 fps and holds the real rate, so a human can count up to 15 flashes a second on it; the
   captures come a few hundred milliseconds apart and show whether the content alternates between
   bright and dark, never how often. Strobe, lightning, gunfire, explosions, rapid cuts or an
   alternation between the captures is a flash indicator for a human to count on the recording with
   PEAT ([G15](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G15)); calm content with one
   luminance in both captures passes. Read the two captures for saturated red as well, since the
   script measures none of these sources. Done when each source has a note.

5. States. Rerun steps 1 to 4 after each state a visitor reaches:

   ```bash
   agent-browser find role button click --name "Accept"
   agent-browser reload
   agent-browser hover "<card css>"
   agent-browser find role button click --name "Menu"
   agent-browser set media dark
   ```

   Also open the first dialog and expanded section, submit a form empty for its error state, step
   through every `motion.js` `carousels[]` entry, start every game or player that needs a click, and
   `reload` for the loading and splash state. The `web page` definition puts every one of these in
   scope even though the URI never changes. Done when every state is in the findings.

6. Evidence. `highlight "<css>"` and `screenshot <path>`; element screenshots of both states; the
   step 4 recording; the step 2 JSON in the findings file. Done when each fail has an image.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): `generalTransitionsPerSecond` 8 or more — at least four flashes a second —
  on an element or `<video>` whose judged area (step 3) is above 21,824 CSS px², with `frames` over
  `secs` of at least 40 — 95. `redTransitionsPerSecond` 8 or more with `maxRed` at or above 0.8 on
  an area above 21,824 CSS px² — 92 (the script already requires both halves of the red-flash
  definition).
- Fail (70–89): `generalTransitionsPerSecond` 8 or more on a source whose inline `areaPx` is at or
  under 21,824 but whose step 3 `fullscreen` is true — 85, judged on `viewportPx`. A flasher above
  21,824 CSS px² alternating into a saturated red seen in the step 4 captures while
  `redTransitionsPerSecond` stays low, because the colour changes at near-constant luminance — 80.
  `generalTransitionsPerSecond` 8 or more with `frames` over `secs` under 40 — 75. Two or more
  sources flashing concurrently above three flashes a second, each under 21,824 CSS px² but with a
  combined contiguous area above 21,824 inside one 341 × 256 region — 75.
- Needs confirmation (50–69): `generalTransitionsPerSecond` or `redTransitionsPerSecond` of exactly
  7 on an area above 21,824 CSS px² — 65; rerun step 2 once, then send the recording to PEAT with
  `minGapMs` quoted, because 7 is either W3C's failing 3.5 flashes (about 143 ms apart) or a
  passing three flashes read one sample early (about 167 ms apart). Two or more separated areas
  flashing concurrently above three flashes a second whose combined area stays under 21,824 — 60;
  G176's "only one area" check fails, so ask the human to run PEAT. A step 4 source whose captures
  alternate between bright and dark or show strobe-like scenes — 60; ask for PEAT on the recording.
  A `<video>` with strobe-like scenes and a transition count of 6 or less, or with windows not
  measured — 55; mean-frame luminance hides a small bright region, ask for a full analysis. An HDR
  or non-sRGB video — 55; record `cannot-tell` and ask for a cd/m² or Michelson analysis. A
  checkerboard or noise pattern whose squares are near 0.1 degree (3 to 5 CSS px) — 55; ask whether
  the exception applies. A candidate in a state the walk did not open — 50; ask whether visitors
  reach it.
- Pass: every candidate satisfies G19 on both checks (at most 6 transitions, hence at most three
  general and three red flashes, in every one-second window, and the window ends in the state it
  began in), or satisfies G176 on both checks (only one area of the screen flashing at a time, and
  that area at or under 21,824 CSS px² at the largest size it can be shown); every step 4 source
  shows calm content across at least two loops; in every state. W3C's own passing examples: "video
  of muzzle flash of machine gun fire, but limits the size of the flashing image to a small portion
  of the screen below the flash threshold size", and "A movie with a scene involving very bright
  lightning flashes is edited so that the lightning only flashes three times in any one second
  period."
- Not present: no video, embed, animated image, canvas or repeating animation in any state.
- False positives to avoid: `motion.js` `blink` counts `<blink>` tags and every computed
  `text-decoration: blink`, so a non-zero count is normal and Chromium animates neither: record a
  note, not a fail. `marquee` is scrolling, not flashing: it belongs to 2.2.2. Fades, slides,
  spinners, progress bars and skeleton shimmer measure 0 or 1 flash. `autoUpdating[]` counts DOM
  mutations, not luminance: a ticker changing text five times a second measures 0. A 16 × 16 px
  indicator (256 CSS px²) is under the area limit however fast it blinks; flashing text flashes only
  its glyph area. Flashers set apart do not add up under the glossary: only the contiguous area
  inside one 341 × 256 region combines — but their concurrency still removes the G176 exception, so
  record them rather than dismissing them. A luminance swing under 10 %, or any swing whose darker
  state is 0.80 or above, is not a flash at any speed. Judder, tearing or banding visible only in
  the recording is display or rendering flicker, which W3C places outside the author's control. The
  text caret, your own hover transitions, `reducedMotionQueryUsed` and a `prefers-reduced-motion`
  rule that stops the flash change nothing: measure in the default media mode.

## Reporting

- File under: 2.3.1 for every source that flashes above the thresholds; the failure invalidates the
  whole page under Conformance Requirement 5 Non-Interference, including a flashing third-party
  advert or embed the author did not author: say so in the issue. Motion longer than 5 seconds
  without a pause control is a separate 2.2.2 issue; sound is 1.4.2. One issue per flashing source;
  the same video, image or animation on several samples is one issue naming them all.
- Title patterns: "Hero video strobes faster than three flashes per second", "Animated banner
  flashes between white and black", "Sale badge blinks more than three times per second", "Red
  flashes in the promo video exceed the red flash threshold".
- Severity: High when the source flashes above the thresholds on load or in a state every visitor
  reaches: the page can trigger a seizure. Medium when it flashes only in a state few visitors open
  (one carousel slide, a video started on request) or only at full screen. Low is not used.
- Type: Content when the flash is inside a video, animated image or embed an editor placed.
  Technical when CSS, script or canvas produces it. Design when the design prescribes the blink.
- Evidence: a page screenshot with the source highlighted; the bright and dark element screenshots
  side by side, or a short GIF from the recording; quote the transitions counted in the worst
  one-second window, the flashes they amount to, the area in CSS px² and the area it was judged at
  (inline or full screen), and whether a saturated red was involved.
- Recommendation: keep every component at three flashes or fewer in any second — six transitions,
  ending in the state it started in — ([G19](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G19)):
  lengthen a CSS cycle, `animation: blink 1s steps(2) infinite` (2 transitions a second, 1 flash) in
  place of `animation: blink .15s steps(2) infinite` (about 13 transitions a second, 6.7 flashes),
  or re-edit the scene the way W3C's G15 example does, adding "a short pause after each pair of
  lightning flashes". Or keep one flashing area under 21,824 CSS px², for example
  `max-width: 160px; max-height: 120px` (19,200 CSS px²), with nothing else flashing at the same
  time and no route to full screen — drop `allowfullscreen` and `allow="fullscreen"` from an iframe
  embed ([G176](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G176)); a `<video>` element cannot
  reliably block the browser's own full-screen control, so reduce its flash count instead. Or cut
  the luminance swing below 10 % of maximum relative luminance, or raise the darker state to 0.80 or
  above, with no saturated red. Verify the result with a tool such as PEAT
  ([G15](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G15)); G19 needs no tool at all. Note for
  the client that readers sitting close to the screen or magnifying it are still affected by areas
  that pass normatively, so G19 is the safer of the two routes.
- Related: 2.2.2 (the same content moving or blinking longer than 5 seconds: one issue for the
  motion, one for the flash; a blink above three times a second goes in both), 1.4.2 (sound from the
  same video), 2.3.2 and 2.3.3 (AAA, evaluated only when the report targets AAA).
