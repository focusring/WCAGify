# 1.4.2 Audio Control

Level A · WCAG 2.0+ · Guideline 1.4 Distinguishable · Group: `motion` (also `media`)

Sources: [Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html)
· [Spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#audio-control)
· [Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/#audio-control)
· [Plain English](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/1-4-2-audio-control/)
· ACT: [80f0bf](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/80f0bf/),
[4c31df](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/4c31df/),
[aaa1bf](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/aaa1bf/)

## Requirement

> If any audio on a web page plays automatically for more than 3 seconds, either a mechanism is
> available to pause or stop the audio, or a mechanism is available to control audio volume
> independently from the overall system volume level.
>
> Note:
> Since any content that does not meet this success criterion can interfere with a user's ability
> to use the whole page, all content on the web page (whether or not it is used to meet other
> success criteria) must meet this success criterion. See Conformance Requirement 5:
> Non-Interference.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary): **mechanism**: "process or technique for achieving a result [...]
Note 2: The mechanism needs to meet all success criteria for the conformance level claimed."
**pause**: "stopped by user request and not resumed until requested by user".

## In plain terms

Sound that starts without the visitor asking for it and runs longer than 3 seconds needs, on the
same page, a way to pause or stop it or to turn its own volume down to zero, independent of the
system volume. Screen reader users are hit hardest: speech output shares the speakers and often
the system volume, and they navigate by listening, so background audio hides the page and the
control that would silence it; people who cannot concentrate on text while sound plays suffer too.
Such sound makes the whole page unusable (Non-Interference). W3C prefers sound started on request.

## Applies when

- Present: sound that starts without the visitor's intentional activation and lasts more than 3
  seconds: an `<audio>` or `<video>` with `autoplay` and no `muted` (`media.js` `media[]` with
  `autoplay` true and `muted` false); a media element a script starts on load, after consent, on
  scroll, on hover or when a state opens; an embedded player whose `src` carries an autoplay
  parameter (`media.js` `kind: 'embed'` with `autoplay` true, `structure.js` `iframes[].src`); a
  sound played through the Web Audio API (both step 5). "Plays automatically" is "audio that is
  not started/played as a direct result of a user's intentional activation" (Understanding): sound
  after a click that did not announce it is automatic.
- Not present: no `media[]` entry with `autoplay` true, no `iframes[].src` with an autoplay
  parameter, and nothing plays after load or after any state change of step 5. Media that plays
  only after a clearly labelled play control is user-requested
  ([G171](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G171)).
- Exceptions: none in the normative text. Out of scope by W3C wording: an element with the
  `muted` attribute or a file with no audio track (ACT 80f0bf applicability); sound that stops
  within 3 seconds and does not loop
  ([G60](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G60)); "a conference call or other
  interaction where two-way voice communication may take place" (Understanding). Muting the system
  or the browser tab is not the mechanism: it must be "independent of the overall system volume"
  (Understanding).

## axe-core

| Rule                | Decides                      | Result → outcome                         |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `no-autoplay-audio` | `controls`; ≤ 3 s, no `loop` | review → step 2; no line for it → step 2 |

`no-autoplay-audio` is on by default and `needs review` only (`reviewOnFail`): its fail state and
a file whose metadata did not load both come back as the one incomplete node whose message is the
check's fail text "<video> or <audio> outputs audio for more than allowed duration and does not
have a controls mechanism" (`reviewOnFail` routes a failed check to `incomplete` but keeps the fail
message; the rule's "Check that ..." incomplete message is never shown). `scripts/axe-page.mjs`
prints only `fail` and `review` lines, so a pass never reaches the agent and every element the rule
sees goes to step 2 anyway. It tests the `muted` attribute, not the property, and neither the audio
track, the visibility of `controls` (hidden elements are included) nor a cross-origin iframe.
Settles: nothing on its own. Leaves open: the audio track; scripted playback without `autoplay`;
embedded players; Web Audio; whether a custom control exists, is visible, named, in the tree and
really pauses, stops or mutes; sound after consent, scroll, hover or a state change; the total
length of looped or chained clips.

## Manual procedure

Chromium may hold unmuted autoplay until the visitor interacts, so `paused` can be true on an
element with `autoplay`: treat the attribute as intent (ACT 80f0bf) and measure. Repeat steps 2
and 3 in every state step 5 opens; the `media` pass re-runs steps 1 to 4 on media samples.

1. Inventory the candidates:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/media.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/structure.js
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   ```

   Keep every `media[]` item with `autoplay` true and `muted` false; every item with `kind:
'embed'`; every `iframes[].src` whose query has `autoplay=1`, `autoplay=true` or
   `auto_play=true` unless `mute=1`, `muted=1` or `background=1` sits beside it (it starts
   silent); and every node axe reported as incomplete. Done when each candidate is in the findings
   file with its `kind` and `selector`.

2. Measure length and audio (ACT aaa1bf). Play every element muted for two seconds, then read:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => Promise.all([...document.querySelectorAll('audio, video')].map(async (m) => {
     const t = /#t=([\d.]*),?([\d.]*)/.exec(m.currentSrc || '');
     m.muted = true; try { await m.play(); } catch (e) {}
     await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 2000)); m.pause();
     const bytes = m.webkitAudioDecodedByteCount ?? null;
     return { src: m.currentSrc, autoplay: m.hasAttribute('autoplay'), loop: m.loop,
       mutedAttr: m.hasAttribute('muted'), controls: m.controls, readyState: m.readyState,
       playable: (t && t[2] ? +t[2] : m.duration) - (t && t[1] ? +t[1] : 0),
       hasAudio: m.mozHasAudio ?? (bytes == null ? null : bytes > 0) };
   })))()
   JS
   agent-browser reload
   ```

   Applicable when `autoplay` true, `mutedAttr` false, `hasAudio` true and `playable` > 3 or
   `loop` true. Drop `hasAudio: false` with `readyState` ≥ 3 (no audio track) and `playable` ≤ 3
   without `loop` ([G60](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G60)). `hasAudio: null`,
   `readyState` < 3 or `playable: NaN` (a cross-origin or unloaded source) is `cannot-tell` unless
   step 3 finds a control. Done when each element is applicable, dropped or `cannot-tell`.

3. Find and operate the control mechanism (ACT 4c31df). `controls` true is the native bar:
   expectation 1 met. Otherwise search the `snapshot -i` output for a button, link, switch or
   slider named for pause, stop, mute, sound or volume, next to the element or at the top of the
   page ([G170](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G170)). Read the state before
   activating the control, activate it, wait, and read again:

   ```bash
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => [...document.querySelectorAll('audio, video')].map((m) =>
     ({ src: m.currentSrc, paused: m.paused, muted: m.muted, volume: m.volume })))()
   JS
   agent-browser find role button click --name "Mute"
   agent-browser wait 5000
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => [...document.querySelectorAll('audio, video')].map((m) =>
     ({ src: m.currentSrc, paused: m.paused, muted: m.muted, volume: m.volume })))()
   JS
   ```

   When the before-read shows `paused: true` on an `autoplay` element, the browser held it: grant
   activation and start it yourself before judging the control.

   ```bash
   agent-browser click "body"
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => document.querySelector('<css of the element>').play())()
   JS
   ```

   Re-read until `paused: false`; only then activate the control. Judge on the difference between
   the two reads: the control counts when `paused` or `muted` turns true or `volume` reaches 0 for
   that element and stays so after the wait ("pause" means "not resumed until requested by user").
   Then expectation 2: the control's `focusables.js` `order[]` entry reads `visible` true, `name`
   not empty, `inAriaHidden` false; confirm with `is visible "<css>"`. A native bar on an `<audio>`
   with `display: none`, and a button that only appears on hover, are not visible. Done when each
   applicable element has a working, visible, named control or a fail indicator with the values.

4. Embedded players. Enter each iframe candidate with `frame @e12` (its ref), `snapshot -i`, then
   `find role button click --name "Mute"` and `frame main`. A player with its own visible, named
   pause or mute button passes; `controls=0` in the `src` with no custom button on the host page,
   or a player snapshot without a pause, mute or volume control, is a fail indicator. The frame
   cannot tell whether the stream has sound: assume it does. Done when each embed has a verdict.

5. Sound that starts without the `autoplay` attribute. Reload, wait, then read what plays and what
   the browser blocked:

   ```bash
   agent-browser reload
   agent-browser wait --load networkidle
   agent-browser wait 5000
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => ({ playing: [...document.querySelectorAll('audio, video')]
        .filter((m) => !m.paused && !m.muted && m.volume > 0)
        .map((m) => ({ src: m.currentSrc, currentTime: m.currentTime, controls: m.controls })),
      soundFiles: performance.getEntriesByType('resource').map((e) => e.name)
        .filter((n) => /\.(mp3|ogg|oga|wav|m4a|aac|flac)(\?|#|$)/i.test(n)) }))()
   JS
   agent-browser console
   agent-browser errors
   ```

   Repeat the read, the console read included, in every state of `recon/<sample>-states.md`, plus
   `scroll down 800` to the end of the page and `hover` on each card or teaser that reacts to
   hover; the cookie dialog is `find role button click --name "Accept"`. A `playing[]` entry is an
   autoplay candidate: run steps 2 and 3 on it. A `NotAllowedError: play() failed because the user
didn't interact with the document first` entry in `console` or `errors` is a scripted autoplay
   attempt the browser blocked: the page is silent here and loud for a visitor who has clicked
   anywhere first. Take its element (an `<audio>` or `<video>` without `autoplay`, `currentSrc`
   set, `paused` true, or the `soundFiles[]` entry that matches a `currentSrc`) as an autoplay
   candidate too: `agent-browser click "body"` grants activation, `play()` through `eval --stdin`
   starts it, then run steps 2 and 3 on it. A `soundFiles[]` entry with no media element is Web
   Audio playback the tree cannot show: record `cannot-tell` with the file name and the state.
   Sound after a control whose name says it plays media is user-requested. Done when every state
   is read with its console.

6. Evidence. `highlight "<player css>"`, then `screenshot <path>` with the player and the place a
   control would sit in view; `screenshot <path> --full` for an `<audio>` without a box; for a
   control that resumes, `record start <path.webm>` before the click and `record stop` after the
   wait. Copy the step 2 and step 3 JSON into the findings file. Done when each fail has an image.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): an `<audio>` or `<video>` with `autoplay`, no `muted` attribute,
  `hasAudio` true, `playable` > 3, no `controls` and no control on the page that sets `paused`,
  `muted` or `volume` 0 ([F93](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F93), ACT 4c31df
  expectation 1) — 95. The same with a control that reads `visible` false or `inAriaHidden` true
  (ACT 4c31df expectation 2) — 90. A control that toggles but the element resumes in 5 s — 90.
- Fail (70–89): playback observed after load, consent, scroll or hover, or a `play()` the browser
  blocked in the console, with no `autoplay` attribute, longer than 3 seconds, no control
  ([F23](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F23)) — 85. An embed with `autoplay=1`,
  no `mute=1` or `muted=1`, `controls=0` and no custom control on the host page — 80. A working
  control whose `name` is empty or whitespace (ACT 4c31df expectation 2) — 80. A clip of 3
  seconds or less with `loop` and no control (ACT aaa1bf leaves repeats open) — 75. A control
  shown only on hover or focus, with no other mechanism — 75. A volume control stuck above 0 — 75.
- Needs confirmation (50–69): `hasAudio: null` or `playable: NaN` with `autoplay` and no control
  — 60; ask whether sound plays and for how long. A `soundFiles[]` entry with no media element
  and no mute control — 55; ask whether a sound plays in that state. A mechanism only on another
  page — 60; ask whether it reaches every page (ACT 80f0bf assumption). A native bar clipped or
  under an overlay — 55; ask whether pause is reachable. Clips chaining past 3 seconds — 60.
- Pass: every element that autoplays audio longer than 3 seconds has, on the same page, a
  visible, named, in-tree control that pauses, stops or mutes it and does not resume; or every
  such sound stops within 3 seconds and does not loop; or no sound starts unrequested.
- Not present: no sound starts on its own, in any state of the sample, for any length.
- False positives to avoid: `muted` autoplay and silent files are inapplicable. `<audio autoplay
controls>` passes (ACT 4c31df passed example 1) although ACT aaa1bf fails on it. `#t=8,10` makes
  `playable` 2 seconds. Two-way voice calls are out of scope. A muted video with an "Unmute"
  button is inapplicable until unmuted. `paused` on load is the browser's policy, not a mechanism.

## Reporting

- File under: 1.4.2 for the missing, hidden or non-working pause, stop or mute mechanism; a
  working control with no accessible name is a 4.1.2 issue that names this criterion (the
  mechanism must meet level A itself); one the keyboard cannot reach is 2.1.1. One issue per
  autoplaying source; the same track on several samples is one issue naming them all.
- Title patterns: "Background music starts on page load and cannot be stopped", "Hero video plays
  sound automatically without a mute control", "Embedded video autoplays with sound and hidden
  player controls", "Sound starts after accepting cookies and has no stop control".
- Severity: High when audio longer than 3 seconds starts on its own with no mechanism on the page,
  or the mechanism is hidden or does not work: the whole page is unusable with a screen reader
  (Non-Interference). Medium when a working mechanism is hard to reach: far down the page, only on
  hover, or on another page. Low when the audio runs only a moment past the limit.
- Type: Technical: remove `autoplay`, add `controls`, or wire a real pause or mute button; also
  when the control is hidden by `display: none` or `aria-hidden`. Design when the control shows
  only on hover or out of view. Content when an editor pasted an embed URL with `autoplay=1`.
- Evidence: a page screenshot with the player highlighted and the place a control would sit in
  view; for a hidden control, an element screenshot of where it renders. Quote the before and
  after `paused`, `muted` and `volume` values. A GIF only when the audio resumes after a pause.
- Recommendation: start sound only on request: drop `autoplay` and keep `controls`
  ([G171](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G171)), or ship
  `<video autoplay muted playsinline>` with a "Turn sound on" button; or put a pause or mute
  control at the top of the page ([G170](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G170));
  or cut the sound to 3 seconds ([G60](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G60)).
- Related: 2.2.2 (the same video moving longer than 5 seconds: one issue for the motion, one for
  the sound), 1.2.2 and 1.2.5 (captions and audio description of the same media), 1.4.7 (AAA),
  4.1.2, 2.1.1, 1.4.11 and 2.4.7 (the control's own name, keyboard access, contrast and focus).
