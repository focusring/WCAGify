# 1.2.4 Captions (Live)

Level AA · WCAG 2.0+ · Guideline 1.2 Time-based Media · Group: `media`

Sources: [Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-live.html) ·
[Spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#captions-live) ·
[Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/#captions-live) ·
[Plain English](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/1-2-4-captions-live/) ·
ACT: none

## Requirement

> Captions are provided for all live
> audio content in synchronized media.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary), quoted because each decides an outcome here:

- **captions**: "synchronized visual and/or text alternative for both speech and non-speech audio
  information needed to understand the media content". Note 1: "[...] captions convey not only the
  content of spoken dialogue, but also equivalents for non-dialogue audio information needed to
  understand the program content, including sound effects, music, laughter, speaker identification
  and location." Note 2: "Closed Captions are equivalents that can be turned on and off with some
  players." Note 3: "Open Captions are any captions that cannot be turned off. For example, if the
  captions are visual equivalent images of text embedded in video." Note 4: "Captions should not
  obscure or obstruct relevant information in the video." Note 5: "In some countries, captions are
  called subtitles."
- **live**: "information captured from a real-world event and transmitted to the receiver with no
  more than a broadcast delay". Note 1: "A broadcast delay is a short (usually automated) delay,
  for example used in order to give the broadcaster time to cue or censor the audio (or video)
  feed, but not sufficient to allow significant editing." Note 2: "If information is completely
  computer generated, it is not live."
- **audio**: "the technology of sound reproduction". Note: "Audio can be created synthetically
  (including speech synthesis), recorded from real world sounds, or both." A synthesised voice or
  a machine-read feed is audio content and needs captions.
- **synchronized media**: "audio or video synchronized with another format for presenting
  information and/or with time-based interactive components, unless the media is a media
  alternative for text that is clearly labeled as such".

## In plain terms

A live broadcast with sound (a webcast, webinar, streamed meeting, concert or news feed) needs
captions produced in real time, burned into the picture or switchable on in the player. Captions
carry the dialogue plus who is speaking and the sounds that matter, so deaf and hard-of-hearing
viewers can follow the event as it happens. W3C aims the criterion at broadcasts, not at two-way
calls between individuals, where the callers own the captions. Producing them is the content
provider's job (a real-time captioner or CART service); the player's job is to show them.

## Applies when

- Present: the sample, in any of its states, plays synchronized media whose audio is live: a
  `<video>` fed by a live stream (HLS `.m3u8`, DASH `.mpd`, a `srcObject`, `duration` equal to
  `Infinity`), an `<iframe>` or `<embed>` of a streaming player (a YouTube or Vimeo live event, a
  webinar, conference, council-meeting or church stream), or a live-event page whose broadcast is
  scheduled ("live at 20:00", a countdown, a "LIVE" badge). Detect with `media.js` (`media[]`:
  `kind`, `selector`, `sources`, `provider`, `duration`, `controls`, `tracks`,
  `textAlternativeNearby`) plus `structure.js` `iframes[]` for a player the media probe missed,
  then step 2.
- Not present: no live synchronized media in any state. Prerecorded video, including the replay of
  a past broadcast, is 1.2.2; a live audio-only stream is 1.2.9 (AAA), not synchronized media.
- Exceptions: none in the normative text, and it says "all live audio content", so one uncaptioned
  stream beside captioned ones still fails. Out of scope by definition:
  - media that is "a media alternative for text that is clearly labeled as such" (the synchronized
    media definition): visible text on the page names the stream an alternative to page text that
    already presents everything the stream does;
  - content that "is completely computer generated" is not live (live, Note 2): a rendered
    simulation, a generated data visualisation or an animated dashboard goes to 1.2.2;
  - two-way calls: the criterion "was intended to apply to broadcast of synchronized media and is
    not intended to require that two-way multimedia calls between two or more individuals through
    web apps must be captioned", and responsibility "would fall to the content providers (the
    callers) or the “host” caller, and not the application";
  - a live feed with no audio track: no success criterion requires captions for silent live video.

## axe-core

No axe-core rule is tagged with this criterion; everything below is manual. The data pack lists no
rule tagged elsewhere that helps here: a clean `agent-browser a11y` run is no evidence for 1.2.4.
No ACT rule is mapped to 1.2.4 either, and the pack holds no advisory technique and no documented
failure, so there is no applicability/expectation pair to follow and no F-number to cite: the
procedure tests for the outcome the sufficient techniques produce, captions arriving while the
broadcast runs.

## Manual procedure

The agent cannot hear the stream. A caption mechanism and arriving cues are observable; accuracy,
completeness and synchronisation against the audio are not: they go to the human (step 6).

1. Inventory the media in the default state, accepting a consent dialog that gates the player:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/media.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/structure.js
   ```

   Keep every `media[]` entry with `kind` `video` or `embed`; note `selector`, `sources`,
   `provider`, `muted`, `controls`, `duration`, `tracks` and `textAlternativeNearby`. Add every
   `structure.js` `iframes[]` entry whose `src` names a streaming host (youtube, vimeo,
   livestream, `/embed/`, `/live/`) that `media.js` missed, and take its `title` as the player's
   name for the findings file. Open the states that reveal a player (a "Watch live" button, a tab,
   a modal) and re-run the probe there. A `textAlternativeNearby` naming a transcript is context,
   not a caption route: carry it to step 4.
   Done when every player on the sample is listed with its selector and the state it lives in.

2. Decide live or prerecorded for each player. Start playback first: before it the flags are
   unreadable (`duration` `NaN`, `readyState` 0). For native video:

   ```bash
   agent-browser find role button click --name "Play"
   agent-browser wait --fn "[...document.querySelectorAll('video')].every(v => v.readyState >= 1)"
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => [...document.querySelectorAll('video')].map(v => ({
     src: v.currentSrc || v.src, live: v.duration === Infinity, srcObject: !!v.srcObject,
     readyState: v.readyState, seekableEnd: v.seekable.length ? v.seekable.end(0) : null,
     tracks: [...v.textTracks].map(t => ({ kind: t.kind, lang: t.language, label: t.label,
       mode: t.mode, cues: t.cues ? t.cues.length : null,
       active: t.activeCues ? t.activeCues.length : null }))
   })))()
   JS
   ```

   `live: true` or `srcObject: true` marks a live stream; a finite `duration` marks it prerecorded
   whatever the source extension (an `.m3u8` or `.mpd` source is a hint only, and hls.js hands out
   a `blob:` `currentSrc`); `readyState` 0 means not loaded: play first, then re-run. Playback that
   never starts: `agent-browser errors` and `agent-browser console` name the blocked autoplay or
   the HLS/DASH failure; a stream that cannot start at all is an off-air player, step 5. For an
   embed, switch into the frame and read its UI:

   ```bash
   agent-browser snapshot
   agent-browser frame @e12
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   agent-browser get text "body"
   agent-browser frame main
   ```

   A "LIVE" badge, a viewer count, no seek slider, "Started streaming" or a YouTube source
   containing `live_stream` marks the embed live; a duration and a seekable progress bar mark it
   prerecorded. Read the text around the player (`get text` on the enclosing section) for "live",
   "webinar", "livestream", a start time or a countdown. A stream running minutes behind the event
   is still live when nothing is edited out: Note 1 allows a delay "not sufficient to allow
   significant editing". Done when each player is tagged `live`, `prerecorded` (hand to 1.2.2) or
   `off-air` (a live page with no broadcast running now).

3. Confirm the live stream carries audio and is a broadcast. For a same-origin `<video>` read the
   decoder twice, five seconds apart: audio that is decoding proves audio content even while the
   element is `muted`.

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => [...document.querySelectorAll('video, audio')].map(m => ({
     tag: m.tagName.toLowerCase(), src: m.currentSrc || m.src,
     mutedAttr: m.hasAttribute('muted'), muted: m.muted, volume: m.volume,
     audioTracks: m.audioTracks ? m.audioTracks.length : null,
     audioBytes: m.webkitAudioDecodedByteCount ?? null, readyState: m.readyState
   })))()
   JS
   agent-browser wait 5000
   ```

   Run the same block again after the wait. `audioTracks` above 0, or an `audioBytes` count that
   rises between the two reads, is audio content. `audioTracks` 0 and `audioBytes` 0 on both reads
   with `readyState` ≥ 3 is a silent feed. A `muted` attribute does not remove audio content: the
   user can unmute it. A cross-origin embed exposes neither number: switch into the frame, take a
   mute or volume control, a "Sound on" prompt or a level meter from the tree as the evidence and
   confirm it with `agent-browser is visible "<mute css>"`. Signs of a two-way call instead of a
   broadcast: camera or microphone prompts (`agent-browser dialog status`), a participant list, a
   "Join" or "Start video" button, `srcObject` on more than one video. Done when the stream is
   tagged `broadcast with audio`, `call` (record `not-present` with the W3C intent quote as the
   reason), `silent feed` (no audio control and nothing to unmute: record `not-present`), or
   `audio unknown` (carry a `cannot-tell` note to step 6).

4. Find the caption mechanism while the broadcast is running. Playback is already started (step
   2); run `agent-browser wait 10000`, then check in this order:
   - Closed captions in native video: switch every caption track on first, because a track without
     `default` sits at `mode` `disabled` with no cues and never fills:

     ```bash
     agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
     (() => { for (const v of document.querySelectorAll('video'))
       for (const t of v.textTracks) if (t.kind === 'captions' || t.kind === 'subtitles')
         t.mode = 'showing'; return 'on'; })()
     JS
     agent-browser wait --fn "[...document.querySelectorAll('video')].some(v =>
       [...v.textTracks].some(t => t.mode !== 'disabled' && t.activeCues && t.activeCues.length))"
     ```

     Cues arrive within about 1.5 s once `mode` is `showing`. A "✗ Wait timed out after 25000ms"
     line is the observation, not a tool error: run the wait a second and a third time (75 s in
     total), or `agent-browser wait 60000`, and re-run the step-2 script. Read `active` only on a
     re-run whose `mode` is other than `disabled`; a track with `kind` `captions` or `subtitles`
     whose `active` count rises is a live caption track. In-band captions (CEA-608/708, WebVTT
     segments in HLS) appear only at runtime, so an empty `tracks` list from `media.js` is not the
     last word.

   - Player captions control: in the tree look for a button named "Captions", "Subtitles" or "CC"
     and activate it (`agent-browser find role button click --name "Subtitles"`); then run
     `agent-browser get text "<player css>"` at two moments 15 s apart. Caption text that changes
     between them is a live feed rendered in the DOM. Native cue rendering sits in the video's
     shadow DOM and stays invisible to `snapshot` and `get text`; the `activeCues` script covers it.

     Worked example, a YouTube live embed: `agent-browser snapshot` for the iframe ref, then

     ```bash
     agent-browser frame @e12
     agent-browser snapshot -i
     agent-browser find role button click --name "Play"
     agent-browser find role button click --name "Subtitles/closed captions"
     agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.2.4-cc-menu.png
     agent-browser frame main
     ```

     The badge reading "LIVE" and a chat panel confirm step 2. A captions button that toggles and
     paints text over the picture is the mechanism; a button that is absent, or a "Settings" menu
     whose "Subtitles/CC" entry reads unavailable, is none. Vimeo names the control "CC".

   - Open captions: `agent-browser screenshot "<player css>" <path>` three times about 10 s apart
     while someone is visibly speaking; burned-in text that changes with the picture is open
     captions ([G93](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G93)), which by Note 3
     "cannot be turned off", so no control is expected.
   - A separate real-time text panel (CART) beside the player counts when its text updates during
     the broadcast ([G9](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G9)). A transcript link
     from step 1 that leads to a page published after the event does not.
   - Obstruction: on the screenshots where captions are visible, compare the caption box with the
     picture. Note 4: "Captions should not obscure or obstruct relevant information in the video."
     Captions covering burned-in speaker names, slides or a score bar are an observation for the
     human (step 6), not a missing mechanism.

   Done when the stream is tagged `open captions`, `closed captions populating`, `captions control
present but no cues after 75 s` or `no caption mechanism`.

5. Handle an off-air player. When the live page shows a placeholder, a countdown or "stream
   offline", the criterion still applies to the page but nothing can be observed now.

   ```bash
   agent-browser get text "<player section css>"
   agent-browser snapshot -i -s "<player section css>"
   agent-browser get attr "time" datetime
   ```

   Read the section text and the idle player's tree for a captions promise ("live captions
   available", "met live ondertiteling", a CC control already in the idle player) and for the
   announced start: a `time` element's `datetime`, a countdown or a date in the text. Done when
   the off-air player has a `cannot-tell` note carrying the re-run time, or a confirmation item
   for the human when no time is announced.

6. Capture evidence and hand off what only a listener can judge. For a running broadcast:

   ```bash
   agent-browser highlight "<player css>"
   agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.2.4-player.png
   agent-browser screenshot "<player controls css>" .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.2.4-cc.png
   agent-browser record start .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.2.4-captions.webm
   agent-browser wait 20000
   agent-browser record stop
   ```

   Start the recording while a speaker is visibly talking, so the clip shows speech without
   captions rather than a still. Queue for the human when captions exist, over two minutes of
   broadcast: do speech and speaker changes get captioned within a few seconds; are the captions in
   the language of the audio; do they carry the non-speech audio the glossary names, "sound
   effects, music, laughter, speaker identification and location"; for a music or performance
   stream, do they identify non-vocal music the way the Understanding's CART example does, "by
   title, movement, composer, and any information that will help the user comprehend the nature of
   the audio"; do the captions cover anything on screen that matters (Note 4). Queue when audio
   presence is unknown: does the feed have sound? Done when the findings file has one line per
   player with its tag, its evidence path and its open questions.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): a running broadcast tagged `broadcast with audio` and `no caption
mechanism`: no text track with cues, no captions control in the player, no burned-in text in
  three screenshots over 30 s, no real-time text panel (90); the page states that its live streams
  are not captioned (95); one live stream on the sample has no caption mechanism while others do —
  "all live audio content" leaves no room for partial coverage (90).
- Fail (70–89): a captions control exists but stays disabled or reports "no captions available"
  during the broadcast (85); a caption track or caption region exists but no cue appears within
  75 s while a speaker is visibly talking (80); captions are offered only as a transcript published
  after the event, which is not synchronized with the live audio (80); the human reports captions
  in a language other than the audio's (75); the human reports captions that miss speech or speaker
  changes for long stretches (75); the human reports dialogue captioned with no equivalent for the
  non-speech audio needed to follow the programme, sound effects, music, laughter or location (75);
  captions stop when the broadcast hands over to a second speaker, a phone-in or a musical item
  (75).
- Needs confirmation (50–69): audio presence is unknown, with no volume control and no player UI
  (60: ask whether the feed has sound); the player looks like a two-way call (55: ask whether it is
  a broadcast or a call between participants); the audio may carry nothing needed to understand the
  picture, such as a webcam with ambient noise (50: ask what the audio contains); the feed runs
  minutes behind and may be edited before transmission rather than broadcast-delayed (55: ask
  whether the feed is cut or edited before it goes out); captions run but sit over burned-in names,
  slides or a score bar, against Note 4 (60: ask whether the covered content is needed).
- Cannot-tell: captions populate, or the player is off-air, and no defect above is observed; record
  `cannot-tell`, and put the step-6 question in the findings Notes and in confirm.md without a fail
  certainty. After the human answers: `pass`, or the fail at the certainty listed above.
- Pass: every live stream with audio on the sample, not only the main one, shows captions during
  the broadcast, open or switched on in the player, cues keep arriving throughout the observed
  window, and the listener confirmed coverage.
- Not present: no live synchronized media in any state of the sample.
- False positives to avoid: a replay of a past broadcast is prerecorded (1.2.2); a live audio-only
  stream is 1.2.9; a `muted` attribute does not remove the audio content; an empty `tracks` list
  from `media.js` is not "no captions", in-band tracks appear at runtime only; captions that are
  off until the viewer switches them on are closed captions, which Note 2 allows ("can be turned on
  and off with some players"); a `kind="subtitles"` track is the caption track when it also names
  speakers and sounds (Note 5: "In some countries, captions are called subtitles"); a short
  broadcast delay does not make a stream prerecorded (Note 1), only editing before transmission
  does; a completely computer-generated stream is not live at all; auto-generated real-time
  captions are allowed ("Captions may be generated using real-time text translation service"),
  judge them on coverage, not on origin; a CART panel beside the player is a valid delivery; a
  captions button without an accessible name is 4.1.2, not a missing mechanism.

## Reporting

- File under: 1.2.4 for a live stream without captions or with captions that fail during the
  broadcast. Prerecorded media goes to 1.2.2; a live audio-only stream to 1.2.9 when AAA is the
  target; a captions control without an accessible name or unreachable by keyboard is 4.1.2 or
  2.1.1; a stream that starts with sound by itself is 1.4.2. One issue per player or streaming
  service; the same service on other samples is one issue that names those samples.
- Title patterns: "Live stream has no captions", "Captions cannot be switched on in the live
  player", "Live webinar captions stop after the introduction", "Only a transcript is published
  after the broadcast"
- Severity: High when the broadcast is the purpose of the page or the only source of the
  information (a meeting, webinar, lecture, news feed) and no captions exist; Medium when captions
  exist but drop out, lag far behind, omit speaker identification and sounds, or run in the wrong
  language; Low when captions take an extra step (a CART page linked from the player) or the feed
  is short and ancillary.
- Type: Content when captions are not produced or are incomplete (a captioning service has to be
  arranged); Technical when they exist but the player cannot show them (no CC support, stripped);
  Design when captions are produced and shown but obscure information in the picture.
- Evidence: page screenshot with `highlight` on the player during the broadcast; a close screenshot
  of the player controls showing no captions control, or the caption menu reading unavailable; and
  the 20 s recording started while a speaker is visibly talking, so the clip shows speech with no
  captions. For an off-air player, the placeholder with its announced start time.
- Recommendation: arrange real-time captioning of the broadcast
  ([G9](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G9)) — a CART captioner or a real-time
  text translation service — and deliver it one of two ways: burned into the picture as open
  captions ([G93](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G93)), or as closed captions the
  viewer switches on ([G87](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G87)) through a
  synchronized text stream in SMIL ([SM11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/smil/SM11),
  [SM12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/smil/SM12)) or "any readily available media
  format that has a video player that supports closed captioning". For HTML5 or HLS that is an
  in-band caption stream (CEA-608/708, WebVTT segments) or a caption track the player exposes:
  ```html
  <video src="https://example.org/live/stream.m3u8" controls>
    <track kind="captions" srclang="nl" label="Nederlands" src="/live/captions.vtt" default />
  </video>
  ```
  The cues have to name the speaker and the non-speech audio, not only the words:
  ```
  WEBVTT

  00:04:12.000 --> 00:04:16.500
  <v Chair>The vote on amendment 7 is now open.

  00:04:16.500 --> 00:04:19.000
  [applause]
  ```
- Related: 1.2.2 for the replay published afterwards; 1.2.9 for an audio-only live feed; 1.4.2 when
  the stream autoplays with sound; 4.1.2 and 2.1.1 for the captions control itself; 1.2.3, 1.2.5
  and 1.2.7 are prerecorded-only, so WCAG asks for no audio description on a live stream.
