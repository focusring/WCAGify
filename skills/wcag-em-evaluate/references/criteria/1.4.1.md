# 1.4.1 Use of Color

Level A · WCAG 2.0+ · Guideline 1.4 Distinguishable · Group: `visual`

Sources: [Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)
· [Spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color)
· [Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/#use-of-color)
· [Plain English](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/1-4-1-use-of-color/)
· ACT: none mapped to this criterion.

## Requirement

> Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an
> action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element.
>
> Note:
> This success criterion addresses color perception specifically. Other forms of perception are
> covered in Guideline 1.3 including programmatic access to color and other visual presentation
> coding.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary): none are linked from the criterion text. The four limbs of the sentence
are the scope, and the Understanding gives an example of each. Conveying information: "required
fields are red", "error is shown in red", "Mary's sales are in red, Tom's are in blue". Indicating
an action: "using color to indicate that a link will open in a new window or that a database entry
has been updated successfully". Prompting a response: "using highlighting on form fields to indicate
that a required field had been left blank". Distinguishing a visual element: a link in running text,
which is "often visually understood only from [its] own display attributes"
([F73](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F73)). What the criterion is aimed at is
"the use of color where each color has a meaning assigned to it".

## In plain terms

Colour may carry meaning, but never on its own: wherever a colour difference tells a sighted user
something, a second visible cue (text, icon, underline, shape, pattern, bold) must say the same —
"Use information in addition to color, such as shape or text, to convey meaning". The people this
serves (colour vision deficiency, partial sight, older users, limited-colour or monochrome displays)
mostly use no assistive technology, so "this criterion requires a visible alternative to color":
`aria-current`, `aria-invalid` or `required` satisfy 1.3.1 and 4.1.2, never this one, and "even if
information that is conveyed by color differences is appropriately conveyed to assistive
technologies, it does not necessarily pass this criterion". A 3:1 lightness difference counts as a
second cue, unless the user has to recognise a particular colour. Colour itself is never the defect:
this "should not in any way discourage the use of color on a page, or even color coding if it is
complemented by other visual indication".

## Applies when

- Present: a colour difference carries meaning in the sample or one of its states: links inside
  running text; required or invalid fields marked by label, border or background colour; a blank
  required field highlighted after submit; the current item of a menu, tab list, pagination or
  calendar; status by a coloured dot, badge, cell, row or word; a success or confirmation message
  carried by a coloured panel; a link marked as opening in a new window by colour; charts, maps and
  diagrams keyed by a colour legend; colour-coded text, schedule tracks or code listings; buttons
  whose meaning is their colour ("green buttons advance in the process and red buttons cancel the
  process"); swatches; focus or hover shown by colour only. Detect with steps 1 to 3 and the
  `links.js`, `forms.js` and `images.js` probes.
- Not present: "This criterion does not apply to situations where color has not been used to convey
  information, indicate an action, prompt a response or distinguish a visual element" — the grey
  pair of step 2 loses nothing, the step 3 script returns no `colourOnly` entry, and the sample has
  no link in running text, required or invalid field, chart, legend, status mark or swatch. A
  distinction no sighted user can see either is out of scope: "There is no requirement that links be
  identifiable by people who cannot perceive color if they are not perceivable for those with color
  vision" (F73; its example is links hidden for everyone, "as in a game or test").
- Exceptions: none normative; four Understanding notes decide the edge cases.
  1. **Lightness.** Colours that differ "not only in their hue, but that also have a significant
     difference in lightness" count as "an additional visual distinction, as long as the difference
     in relative luminance between the colors leads to a contrast ratio of 3:1 or greater". W3C's
     examples: "a light green and a dark red differ both by color (hue) and by lightness, so they
     would pass if the contrast ratio is at least 3:1", and "if content is distinguished by
     inverting an element's foreground and background colors, this would pass (again, assuming that
     the foreground and background colors have a sufficient contrast)". F73 adds the same-hue case —
     "red and pink would pass the requirement for 'not distinguished by color (hue) alone' since
     they differ by lightness [...] as long as the difference in lightness (contrast) is 3:1 or
     greater" — measured "before focus or pointing".
  2. **A particular colour.** "However, if content relies on the user's ability to accurately
     perceive or differentiate a particular color an additional visual indicator will be required
     regardless of the contrast ratio between those colors. For example, knowing whether an outline
     is green for valid or red for invalid." A 3:1 lightness difference does not rescue a
     red-for-invalid / green-for-valid pair, a red-versus-green status, or any cue whose meaning is
     which colour it is rather than that the colours differ.
  3. **No colour differentiation at all.** "A hyperlink which has been styled to appear no different
     than neighboring static text would not fail this success criterion, as there would be no color
     differentiation between the actionable hyperlink text and the adjacent static text." Record the
     usability problem W3C names ("poor usability for anyone looking at the interface") in the
     findings file, not as a 1.4.1 fail.
  4. **Visited links.** "Where color alone distinguishes between visited and unvisited links, it
     does not result in a failure of this Success Criterion, even where the contrast between the two
     link colors is below 3:1", because "setting or conveying a link's visited status is not an
     author responsibility". Both link colours must still clear 1.4.3: "authors must continue to
     ensure that all text links meet contrast minimums against the page background (SC 1.4.3)".

## axe-core

| Rule                 | Decides                | Result → outcome                            |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `link-in-text-block` | link cue beyond colour | violation → fail (100); incomplete → step 4 |

Default rule set (WCAG 2.0 Level A & AA), impact serious. Scope: selector `a[href], [role=link]`
with `excludeHidden: false`, matched only when the element's trimmed `innerText` is non-empty, any
explicit `role` is `link`, the element is visible on screen, and it sits in a block that holds other
text. An icon-only link, a `role="button"` link, a link alone in its own block and a hidden link
never match: they go to step 4 or step 8.

The rule passes when either of its two checks passes. The style check passes on a non-colour style
difference from the enclosing block: `background-image` other than `none`; a `border-bottom`,
`border-top` or `outline` whose style is not `none`, whose width is above 0 and whose colour is not
fully transparent; a different first font family; or a different `text-decoration-line`,
`text-decoration-style`, `font-weight`, `font-style` or `font-size`. A left or right border, and a
border that only changes colour, do not count. The contrast check (shipped options
`requiredContrastRatio: 3`, `allowSameColor: true`) passes when the link text is at 3:1 or
more against the block's text colour, or the link background at 3:1 or more against the block's
background, each ratio floored to two decimals
([G183](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G183)); it also passes when link and block
match on both text and background (both ratios exactly 1 — `allowSameColor`, note 3 above).

Read the outcome from `failureSummary` on the saved node: agent-browser's `a11y --json` keeps the
rendered message, not axe's `data` object, so the numbers arrive as text. A fail reads "The link has
insufficient color contrast of <ratio>:1 with the surrounding text. (Minimum contrast is 3:1, link
text: <hex>, surrounding text: <hex>)" (message key `fgContrast`), or "The link background has
insufficient color contrast of <ratio> ..." with the two background hex values (`bgContrast`, used
only when link and block text colours have the same luminance and the backgrounds differ). Take the
ratio and both hex values straight into the issue. An incomplete reads one of: "Element's foreground
contrast ratio could not be determined", "Element's background contrast ratio could not be
determined", "... due to a background image", "... due to a background gradient", "... because
element contains an image node", "... because of element overlap", or, from the style check, "Check
if the link's pseudo style is sufficient to distinguish it from the surrounding text" (the link
carries `::before`/`::after` content, which axe cannot weigh).

Settles: every link the matcher takes. Leaves open: links outside text blocks and links with no text
of their own; required, invalid, status and current-item marks; charts, legends, maps, colour-coded
text, swatches; whether a lightness pass is enough (notes 1 and 2); colour-only focus and hover;
instructions that name a colour; success, error and new-window markers; anything inside an image.

## Manual procedure

Visual mode, `set viewport 1280 900`, the sample as loaded, then every recorded state (menu open,
dialog, error state) with `snapshot` again after each change; repeat steps 2 and 3 per state and let
steps 4 to 8 judge what they produce. Evidence per fail: `highlight "<css>"`, `screenshot <path>` in
colour and again with the filter on.

1. Axe results for this sample and each state.

   ```bash
   jq -r '(.result.violations // [])[], (.result.incomplete // [])[]
     | select(.id == "link-in-text-block")
     | "nodes \(.nodeCount)",
       (.nodes[] | "  \(.target[0])  \(.failureSummary | gsub("\n"; " | "))")' \
     .notes/audit/axe/<sample>.json
   ```

   Each violation node is a fail (100) with its `target` selector, its ratio and the two hex colours
   from the message; each incomplete node goes on the step 4 list with the reason its message names.
   A `nodeCount` above the number of nodes listed means axe printed at most ten: rerun
   `scripts/axe-page.mjs --selector "<css>"` on that subtree before deciding. Done when every node
   is either a recorded fail or on the step 4 list.

2. Greyscale pair: with colour filtered out every colour-only cue vanishes; what still tells the
   items apart is shape, text, position or lightness.

   ```bash
   agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.4.1-colour.png --full
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'EOF'
   (() => { document.documentElement.style.filter = 'grayscale(1)'; return true })()
   EOF
   agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.4.1-grey.png --full
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'EOF'
   (() => { document.documentElement.style.filter = ''; return true })()
   EOF
   ```

   The filter sits on the root element only, so every descendant keeps its computed colours: steps 3
   to 8 read the same values with it on or off, and no `reload` is needed (a reload would drop the
   state). List every place the colour image says something the grey one does not: which item is
   current, which field is required or wrong, which words are links, what a legend keys, which
   status a row or dot has, which panel is the success message, which button advances and which
   cancels. Done when each is a candidate with a `snapshot` selector, or the pair loses nothing.

3. State script: compares every current, selected, pressed, checked, invalid, required or
   error-classed element with an unmarked sibling of the same tag and lists what differs.

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'EOF'
   ;(() => {
     const props = ['color', 'backgroundColor', 'borderColor', 'textDecorationLine', 'fontWeight',
       'fontStyle', 'fontSize', 'textTransform', 'borderStyle', 'borderWidth', 'outlineStyle',
       'boxShadow'], colour = props.slice(0, 3)
     const sides = ['borderTopColor', 'borderRightColor', 'borderBottomColor', 'borderLeftColor']
     const lum = (c) => (c.match(/\d+(\.\d+)?/g) || [0, 0, 0]).slice(0, 3).map((n) =>
       (n /= 255) <= 0.04045 ? n / 12.92 : ((n + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4)
       .reduce((s, v, i) => s + v * [0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722][i], 0)
     const ratio = window.__ratio141 = (a, b) =>
       +((Math.max(lum(a), lum(b)) + 0.05) / (Math.min(lum(a), lum(b)) + 0.05)).toFixed(2)
     const alpha = (c) => +((c.match(/[\d.]+/g) || [])[3] ?? 1)
     const sel = (el) => el.localName + (el.id ? `#${CSS.escape(el.id)}`
       : el.classList.length ? `.${CSS.escape(el.classList[0])}` : '')
     const glyphs = (el) => el.querySelectorAll('svg, img, [class*="icon"]').length +
       ['::before', '::after'].filter((p) =>
         !/^(none|normal|"")$/.test(getComputedStyle(el, p).content)).length
     const compare = (el, ref) => {
       const a = getComputedStyle(el), b = getComputedStyle(ref)
       const differs = props.filter((p) => a[p] !== b[p])
       return { selector: sel(el), text: (el.textContent || el.value || '').trim().slice(0, 60),
         differs, colourOnly: differs.length > 0 && differs.every((p) => colour.includes(p)) &&
         glyphs(el) === glyphs(ref),
         appears: [...sides, 'backgroundColor'].filter((p) => !alpha(a[p]) !== !alpha(b[p])),
         fgRatio: ratio(a.color, b.color), bgRatio: ratio(a.backgroundColor, b.backgroundColor),
         borderRatio: Math.max(1, ...sides.flatMap((s) => a[s] === b[s] ? [] : ratio(a[s], b[s]))) }
     }
     const marked = '[aria-current]:not([aria-current="false"]), [aria-selected="true"], ' +
       '[aria-pressed="true"], [aria-checked="true"], [aria-invalid="true"], [required], ' +
       '.active, .selected, .current, .is-active, .error, .invalid, .required'
     const out = [...document.querySelectorAll(marked)].flatMap((el) => {
       const pool = el.matches('input, select, textarea') ? document.querySelectorAll(el.localName)
         : el.parentElement?.children ?? []
       const ref = [...pool].find((s) => s !== el && s.localName === el.localName &&
         !s.matches(marked))
       if (!ref || !el.getClientRects().length) return []
       const r = compare(el, ref), c = el.firstElementChild, d = ref.firstElementChild
       return [r.differs.length || !c || !d ? r : compare(c, d)]
     })
     return { count: out.length, colourOnly: out.filter((o) => o.colourOnly), all: out }
   })()
   EOF
   ```

   `colourOnly` is true when only `color`, `backgroundColor` or `borderColor` differ and no icon or
   generated content was added; the ratios are the lightness contrast of the two values, and one at
   3 or more passes on note 1 — unless the user has to tell which colour it is, where note 2 asks
   for a second indicator "regardless of the contrast ratio". A non-empty `appears` names a border,
   underline or fill drawn where the sibling has none: a shape the grey shot still shows, so pass it
   unless the grey pair cannot tell the two apart. `differs` empty on a `[required]` field means no
   visual mark at all: step 5. Unlisted step 2 candidates are judged from the pair. Done when each
   entry is decided or handed to step 5, 6, 7 or 8.

4. Links in running text. (a) Each step 1 incomplete: `get styles "<link css>"` and
   `get styles "<block css>"`, then `__ratio141('<link color>', '<block color>')` through
   `eval --stdin` (the helper step 3 leaves on `window`). A `text-decoration-line`,
   `border-bottom-style`, `background-color`, `font-weight`, `font-style`, `font-size` or
   `font-family` that differs from the block is the cue — F73: "If the link is a different color and
   bold it would not fail because the boldness is not color dependent", and its procedure accepts
   "underlined, bolded, italicized, sufficient difference in lightness"
   ([G182](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G182) adds "changes to the font size").
   Otherwise a ratio at 3 or more passes on lightness (G183) and below 3 fails (F73). G183 measures
   link text against the surrounding non-link text, not against the background — record both text
   colours so the `visual` pass can also check each against the page background for 1.4.3.
   (b) Each `links.js` entry with `context` `sentence` or `paragraph` in neither axe list: the same,
   judged from the pair when the colours sit in an image. (c) `listitem`, `tablecell`, `heading` or
   `none` context (menus, cards, button rows): identified by position, no cue needed, unless
   `contextText` is running prose around the link — then it takes (b). A cue only after `hover` or
   `focus` does not rescue a link ("it is still a failure", F73). Done when every link in running
   text is pass or fail.

5. Required and invalid fields ([F81](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F81)).
   Run `forms.js`; `ariaRequired` and `ariaInvalid` hold the raw attribute, so test them against
   `"true"` — `"false"` is a truthy string. For each field with `required` or `ariaRequired`
   `"true"`, look at its label and border in the colour screenshot: F81's first example is the
   label "Phone Number" "displayed in a color different from the color used for optional fields,
   without any other indication". A label or border that differs from the optional fields by colour
   alone, with no asterisk, "(required)" text or icon in `visibleLabelText` or beside the field,
   fails. Two things rescue it, both from F81's note: text "sufficiently different in visual
   presentation (e.g. bold or in a different font)", or "sufficient luminosity difference
   (lightness) from the other text [...] A minimum contrast ratio of 3:1 is considered sufficient" —
   compare with `__ratio141` and pass this criterion, then record that the field "may still fail
   other requirements, such as 1.3.1, 3.3.1, and 4.1.2" for those passes. A text cue counts only
   where it is part of the control's name.
   [G205](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G205): "The text cue must be included as
   part of the programmatically determinable name for the control" — so check it appears in the
   `forms.js` `name`, not only in loose text nearby. It also counts only where it is unconditional:
   [G14](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G14) requires "the text is not conditional
   content", so a cue that lives in `title`, in a tooltip or in text revealed on focus does not
   count. `counts.requiredWithoutIndication` points at candidates, the screenshot
   decides. Error state: load it, or `click` submit on the empty form and `wait 500`, run `forms.js`
   again; F81's second example is a field "indicated by red text only, without an additional
   non-color indication". Each field whose border, label or background is recoloured needs a
   `visibleErrors` entry whose `forField` is it, or an icon or text at the field; a field with
   `ariaInvalid` `"true"` and no visual mark at all is 3.3.1. Done when every field marked by colour
   has a non-colour mark or a fail.

6. Charts, maps and legends ([G111](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G111),
   [F13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F13)). Run `images.js`; take entries
   with `presentational` false and `visible` true whose `src`, `name`, `figcaption` or
   `nearbyText` suggests a chart, map, diagram, legend or key, plus any image the pair flagged.
   `screenshot "<css>" <path>` and read it; `get text "<css>"` lists an inline `svg`'s labels.
   Series, regions and keys need labels, values, patterns, shapes or line styles beside the colour,
   and the non-colour cue has to be on the data _and_ in the key: G111's bar chart gives each bar "a
   different solid color and a different pattern" and "The legend uses the same colors and patterns
   to identify each bar"; its map marks the stops of each route "with a distinctive icon such as a
   diamond, square, or circle"; its flow chart uses "dashed, arrowed lines with a green background"
   against "dotted arrowed lines with a red background". The Understanding's own pass is the
   examination: an SVG whose elements carry numbers, where "A legend shows the color and number for
   each type of element" — a colour-keyed graphic passes when the key's second cue is repeated on
   every datum. When `alt`, `name` and `figcaption` also omit what the colour conveys, add an F13
   fail under 1.1.1: F13's example alt ends "The red bars indicate sales that were below the yearly
   quota" and fails because "The alternative should indicate which people did not meet the sales
   quota rather than relying on color". Swatches need the colour name in visible text. In a `canvas`
   or a raster image whose legend cannot be read, record `cannot-tell`. Done when each graphic is
   decided.

7. Instructions, status and action cues (G14, G182). Collect the sentences that name a colour and
   the panels that carry one.

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'EOF'
   (() => {
     const words = ['red', 'green', 'blue', 'yellow', 'orange', 'purple', 'pink', 'grey', 'gray',
       'amber', 'highlighted', 'coloured', 'colored', 'in colour', 'in color']
     const re = new RegExp('\\b(' + words.join('|') + ')\\b', 'i'), out = []
     const walk = document.createTreeWalker(document.body, NodeFilter.SHOW_TEXT)
     for (let n = walk.nextNode(); n; n = walk.nextNode()) {
       const t = (n.data || '').trim(), el = n.parentElement
       if (t.length < 4 || !re.test(t) || !el || !el.getClientRects().length) continue
       if (el.closest('script, style')) continue
       out.push({ selector: el.localName + (el.id ? `#${CSS.escape(el.id)}` : ''),
         text: t.slice(0, 160) })
     }
     return out
   })()
   EOF
   ```

   An instruction that names only the colour fails (G14: the information must be "available in
   text"). It passes when it also names the non-colour cue — G14's form says "all required fields
   are displayed in red and marked with an asterisk *" — or when it names the control by its label
   rather than its colour: "Press the button labeled Go to submit your results". Then take the
   status and action markers the pair flagged: a success, warning or error panel needs a word or an
   icon of its own, not just a green or red fill; a `links.js` entry with `opensNewWindow` true
   whose only difference from the other links is colour fails the "indicating an action" limb;
   scattered colour-coded words in prose need a style difference too (G182: "changes to the font
   style, the addition of underlines, bold, or italics, or changes to the font size"). G182's note
   also applies: a style cue satisfies this criterion, while "the information conveyed by the color
   must also be available programmatically to satisfy Success Criterion 1.3.1". Done when every
   sentence in the list and every status or action marker is pass, fail or a question for the human.

8. Focus and hover by colour only. `nontext-contrast.js` `focusIndicators` says which focusables
   gain an outline at all; measure the rest here.

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/nontext-contrast.js
   agent-browser mouse move 0 0
   agent-browser get styles "<css>"
   agent-browser focus "<css>"
   agent-browser get styles "<css>"
   agent-browser hover "<css>"
   agent-browser get styles "<css>"
   ```

   `mouse move 0 0` first, because a hover persists. Feed the rest and focused or hovered
   `background-color` pairs to `__ratio141`. A focus or hover state whose only change is colour, at
   a ratio below 3, fails here as well as under 2.4.7 and 1.4.11; an added outline, underline,
   border or icon passes ([C15](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C15) is the advisory
   technique for this). A cue that appears only on hover or focus never rescues the resting state of
   a link or a field (F73, step 4). Done when every interactive element the pair flagged has its
   resting and focused states measured.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): `link-in-text-block` violation (100); a link in running text measured in
  step 4 below 3:1 with no other style difference (95, F73); a cue whose meaning is which colour it
  is — red for invalid against green for valid, red against green status — with no second indicator,
  whatever its ratio (90, note 2); an `[aria-current]`, `[aria-selected]`, `[aria-checked]`,
  `[aria-pressed]` or `[aria-invalid]` entry with `colourOnly` true, `appears` empty, every ratio
  below 3 and the grey shot unable to tell it apart (90); a required or invalid field marked by
  colour alone with no `visibleErrors` entry, icon or text at it (90, F81).
- Fail (70–89): a class-based state entry with `colourOnly` true, ratios below 3, and the grey
  image cannot tell the item apart (85); a chart, map or diagram keyed by a colour legend with no
  labels, values, patterns or shapes on the data (80, G111 not applied), and an alt that omits it
  too (80 under 1.1.1, F13); a status dot, cell, row, badge or word with no text or icon, or a
  swatch with no visible name (80, G14 not applied); a link only the pair shows as colour-only
  (75); focus or hover by a colour change below 3:1 (75); an instruction naming only a colour (75);
  a text cue that is conditional content — in `title`, in a tooltip, or revealed only on focus (75,
  G14); a success, warning or new-window marker carried by a coloured fill with no word or icon
  (75); a required-field cue present as loose text but absent from the control's name (70, G205).
- Needs confirmation (50–69): coloured text, cells or rows of unclear meaning, brand or warning
  (60): ask what the colour conveys; a state passing on lightness where the user may have to name
  the colour, green free and red taken (65): ask whether a specific colour must be perceived; a
  legend that carries a pattern, shape or number the data does not, or the reverse (65): ask which
  one the reader is meant to match; colour-coded content in a `canvas` or raster image whose legend
  cannot be read (50, cannot-tell).
- Pass: every candidate from the pair has a visible non-colour cue, or a 3:1 lightness difference
  where only a difference must be noticed; a link, label or word set apart by bold, italic, a
  different font family or a larger size (F73, G182); a chart whose key's second cue is repeated on
  every datum (Understanding Example 2); a required or error field in bold, in a different font or
  at 3:1 lightness, noting that this "may still fail other requirements, such as 1.3.1, 3.3.1, and
  4.1.2" (F81); every link in running text passes axe or step 4; every required and invalid field,
  chart, legend, swatch, instruction, status marker and colour-only focus change has its cue.
- Not present: the pair loses nothing, no `colourOnly` entry, no inline link, field mark or chart;
  or the distinction is invisible to everyone (note 3, F73's hidden links).
- False positives to avoid: a coloured badge containing its status word ("Active"); colour coding
  that is complemented by another visible cue; a transparent border or fill that gains a colour
  (`appears`: the line itself is the cue); inverted foreground and background at sufficient contrast
  (note 1); visited-link colour, even below 3:1 between the two link colours (note 4); a link styled
  identical to its text (note 3, not applicable); links in menus, cards and button rows (marked by
  position); an instruction that names the control's label rather than its colour; an `aria-hidden`
  icon; a lightness pass without a hover cue; a missing programmatic state.

## Reporting

- File under: 1.4.1 for the missing visible cue; a chart alt that omits the colour-coded values is
  a second issue under 1.1.1 (F13 names both); an error with no error text anywhere is 3.3.1 with
  the colour mark part of it, error text present but the field marked by colour alone is 1.4.1.
  One issue per pattern (all body links, all required fields, the menu), naming other samples.
- Title patterns: "Links in body text are distinguished by colour only", "Required fields are

- Recommendation: keep the colour, add the visible cue. Links: restore the underline, or keep link

text-decoration: none; }` clears 3:1 against black non-link text and 4.5:1 against a white
  background, and its #3366CC on black measures 3.9:1. G183 still advises an underline or bold on
