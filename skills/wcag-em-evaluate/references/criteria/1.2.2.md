# 1.2.2 Captions (Prerecorded)

Level A · WCAG 2.0+ · Guideline 1.2 Time-based Media · Group: `media`

Sources: [Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html)
· [Spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#captions-prerecorded)
· [Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/#captions-prerecorded)
· [Plain English](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/1-2-2-captions-prerecorded/)
· ACT: [f51b46](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/f51b46/),
[eac66b](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/eac66b/),
[ab4d13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/ab4d13/)

## Requirement

> Captions are provided for all prerecorded
> audio content in synchronized media, except when the media is a media alternative for text and
> is clearly labeled as such.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary): **captions**: "synchronized visual and/or text alternative for both
speech and non-speech audio information needed to understand the media content". Note 1: captions
"convey not only the content of spoken dialogue, but also equivalents for non-dialogue audio
information needed to understand the program content, including sound effects, music, laughter,
speaker identification and location" — this sentence is the F8 test. Note 2: "Closed Captions are
equivalents that can be turned on and off with some players." Note 3: "Open Captions are any
captions that cannot be turned off. For example, if the captions are visual equivalent images of
text embedded in video." Note 4: "Captions should not obscure or obstruct relevant information in
the video." Note 5: "In some countries, captions are called subtitles" — so a `subtitles` track can
be the caption route. Note 6: "Audio descriptions can be, but do not need to be, captioned".

**synchronized media**: "audio or video synchronized with another format for presenting information
and/or with time-based interactive components, unless the media is a media alternative for text
that is clearly labeled as such" — audio with no second format is 1.2.1, not this criterion.
**audio**: "the technology of sound reproduction"; its note adds that audio "can be created
synthetically (including speech synthesis), recorded from real world sounds, or both", so a
synthesised voice-over is audio content. **prerecorded**: "information that is not live"; **live**
(reached through it): "information captured from a real-world event and transmitted to the receiver
with no more than a broadcast delay [...] If information is completely computer generated, it is
not live." **media alternative for text**: "media that presents no more information than is already
presented in text (directly or via text alternatives)"; its note allows it to be "audio-only,
video-only (including sign-language video), or audio-video".

## In plain terms

Every prerecorded video with a soundtrack needs captions: timed text carrying the dialogue and the
non-speech sounds a viewer needs (who speaks, music, laughter, sound effects), closed (a `<track>`
or a player control the viewer switches on) or open (burned into the picture), so people who are
deaf or hard of hearing can follow it. W3C's intent is that captions "not only include dialogue,
but identify who is speaking and include non-speech information conveyed through sound, including
meaningful sound effects", so dialogue-only text is not yet captions. The one exception is a video
that only repeats the page text and is clearly labeled as such; 1.2.1 covers silent video and
audio-only, 1.2.4 live video.

## Applies when

- Present: a visible, playable video with prerecorded audio that is not silence: a `<video>`, an
  embedded player (`<iframe>`, `<object>`, `<embed>` from YouTube, Vimeo or another host) or a
  scripted player, in any state. Detect with `media.js` (`media[]` `kind`, `selector`, `provider`,
  `controls`, `duration`, `poster`, `tracks[]`, `textAlternativeNearby`) and `structure.js`
  `iframes[]`. A video whose audio is only music counts: the orchestra example in the Understanding
  captions the music by "title, movement, composer, and any information that will help the user
  comprehend the nature of the audio".
- Not present: no state of the sample has a video with audio; `<audio>` and silent video are 1.2.1.
- Exceptions: "except when the media is a media alternative for text and is clearly labeled as
  such": visible text beside the video says it presents the same information as the page text and
  the page text contains all the video conveys (ACT ab4d13, step 5). W3C states it as "Captions are
  not needed when the synchronized media is, itself, an alternate presentation of information that
  is also presented via text on the web page" — its examples are a legal document whose clips show
  a person speaking the contents of each paragraph, each clip associated with its paragraph, and a
  manual whose clip shows a part in the orientation the text describes.
- Not an exception: a recent or time-sensitive video. W3C only acknowledges "that at the present
  time there may be difficulty in creating captions for time-sensitive material"; it grants no
  exemption, so an uncaptioned news clip published yesterday still fails. A live stream is 1.2.4.

## axe-core

| Rule            | Decides                                   | Result → outcome                 |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| `video-caption` | `<video>` has a `<track kind="captions">` | incomplete: step 3; pass: step 4 |

`video-caption` is in the "WCAG 2.0 Level A & AA Rules" set, so `agent-browser a11y` runs it by
default; it maps to ACT eac66b. Settles: nothing; the rule never reports a violation (`issueType`
is `needs review`) and a pass only proves a track element exists. It looks at `<video>` elements in
the main document and same-origin frames only. Leaves open: whether the video has audio; open
(burned-in) captions; whether the track loads, has cues and covers the audio (F8); cross-origin
iframes (YouTube, Vimeo), `<object>`, `<embed>` and scripted players; SMIL text streams; the
exception and its label (F74, F75); a later state.

## Manual procedure

1. Inventory the players in every state of the sample.

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/media.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/structure.js
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   ```

   Take every `media[]` entry of `kind` `video` or `embed` (`audio` goes to 1.2.1) and every
   `iframes[]` entry whose `src` names a video host (youtube, vimeo, wistia, `/embed/`) the probe
   missed. For each button, link or poster named like "Play": `click` it, `wait 1500`, re-run
   `media.js`, add it, `press Escape`. Repeat that for the states that hide media: a lightbox or
   dialog, a tab panel, an accordion, each carousel slide, and the page behind a cookie or consent
   wall (accept it, `reload`, re-run `media.js` — a blocked embed reports no `tracks[]` at all).
   Done when each player has a selector, or none: `not-present`.

2. Confirm each player is visible, prerecorded and has audio. ACT eac66b applies to "every
   non-streaming video element that is visible, where the video contains audio"; f51b46 narrows it
   to audio "that is not only silence". Both assume "the video element is used to play a video (for
   example, not only used to display an image), and that there is a mechanism to start the video".

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (async () => { const out = [];
     for (const v of document.querySelectorAll('video')) {
       const m = v.muted; v.muted = true;
       try { await v.play(); } catch {}
       await new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, 3000)); v.pause(); v.muted = m;
       out.push({ src: v.currentSrc, duration: v.duration, readyState: v.readyState,
         audioBytes: v.webkitAudioDecodedByteCount ?? null, audioTracks: v.audioTracks?.length });
     }
     return out; })()
   JS
   ```

   Hidden (`is visible "<selector>"` false): skip unless a state reveals it. `duration` `0` or
   `NaN` with no `src`: only a poster, drop it. `duration` `Infinity`: live, hand it to 1.2.4.
   `audioBytes` `0` (or `audioTracks` `0`) with `readyState` ≥ 3 after 3 s: silent, hand it to
   1.2.1. Done when each player is marked audio yes, no or unknown; unknown counts as yes when the
   snapshot (inside `frame "<iframe selector>"` for an embed) shows a volume or mute control or
   `controls` is true in `media.js`, otherwise record `cannot-tell` (55): "has it a soundtrack?".

3. Find the caption route of each player with audio (ACT f51b46; techniques
   [H95](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H95),
   [G93](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G93),
   [G87](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G87)).

   - Track (a `tracks[]` entry of `kind` `captions` or `subtitles`): load it as the browser does.

     ```bash
     agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
     (async () => { const out = [], sel = 'track[kind="captions"], track[kind="subtitles"]';
       const sec = s => s.split(':').reduce((a, b) => a * 60 + +b.replace(',', '.'), 0);
       for (const t of document.querySelectorAll(sel)) {
         t.track.mode = 'hidden';
         let text = '', ok = false;
         try { const r = await fetch(t.src); ok = r.ok; text = await r.text(); } catch {}
         await new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, 2000));
         const c = [...text.matchAll(/([\d:.,]+) --> ([\d:.,]+)/g)]
           .map(m => [sec(m[1]), sec(m[2])]);
         out.push({ track: t.src, kind: t.kind, srclang: t.srclang, label: t.label, httpOk: ok,
           cues: c.length, trackReadyState: t.readyState, loadedCues: t.track.cues?.length ?? 0,
           isVtt: /^WEBVTT/.test(text), duration: t.closest('video')?.duration,
           crossorigin: t.closest('video')?.crossOrigin ?? null,
           covered: c.reduce((a, [s, e]) => a + e - s, 0),
           maxGap: Math.max(0, ...c.slice(1).map((s, i) => s[0] - c[i][1])),
           speakerOrSound: /\[[^\]]+\]|\([^)]+\)|<v |^[A-Z][^\n:]{1,30}:/m.test(text),
           sample: text.replace(/\s+/g, ' ').slice(0, 300) });
       }
       return out; })()
     JS
     ```

   - Open captions: seek to 25 %, 50 % and 75 % and screenshot the frame each time. Disable every
     text track first, or a `showing` track is photographed and counted as burned-in text.

     ```bash
     for f in 25 50 75; do
       agent-browser eval --stdin <<JS
       (async () => { const v = document.querySelector('<video selector>');
         [...v.textTracks].forEach(t => { t.mode = 'disabled'; });
         v.muted = true; try { await v.play(); } catch {}
         v.currentTime = v.duration * 0.$f;
         await new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, 1500)); v.pause(); return v.currentTime; })()
     JS
       agent-browser screenshot "<video selector>" \
         .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.2.2-f$f.png
     done
     ```

     The heredoc delimiter is unquoted here so `$f` expands; the body has no other `$`. Text along
     the lower edge of all three frames with every track disabled is open captions (Note 3 counts
     "visual equivalent images of text embedded in video"). Same three frames per slide or chapter
     when the video is a playlist.

   - Player captions (embed or scripted player): switch the player's own captions on. A scripted
     player (JW Player, Wistia, Kaltura, Plyr, Able Player) draws captions with no `<track>` element
     and keeps them off until switched on, so the frames above stay empty until this branch has run.

     ```bash
     agent-browser frame "<iframe selector>"        # embed only; skip it for a scripted player
     agent-browser snapshot -i
     agent-browser find role button click --name "Play"
     agent-browser find role button click --name "Captions"
     agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--1.2.2-player.png
     agent-browser frame main
     ```

     A button named "Captions", "Subtitles", "CC" or "Subtitles/closed captions" (YouTube; Vimeo
     names it "CC") is the caption route: click it, then take the 25/50/75 % frames again. A button
     that is absent or disabled means no captions; an entry ending in "(auto-generated)" under
     "Settings" then "Subtitles/CC" is machine captions. A cookie wall or "video unavailable" in the
     frame: `tab new`, `open <iframe src>`.

   - SMIL text stream ([SM11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/smil/SM11) for SMIL 1.0,
     [SM12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/smil/SM12) for SMIL 2.0): an `<object>`,
     `<embed>` or link whose `src` ends in `.smil`. `get attr "<selector>" src`, then
     `agent-browser read <src>`, and look inside the `<par>` that holds the `<video>` for a
     `<textstream>` (SM11) or `<text>` (SM12) element in a second caption `region`. Both techniques
     are tested the same way: enable the caption preference in the player if it has one, play, and
     check whether captions are displayed — so still take the three frames.

   Done when each player is marked `track`, `open`, `player` (human or auto-generated), `smil` or
   `none`, and the choice is recorded per player in the findings file.

4. Check coverage and content (F8; f51b46 expectation: "audio information that is not conveyed
   visually in the video, is available through captions"). `trackReadyState` 3 or `loadedCues` 0:
   the track shows nothing, 2 is loaded (`httpOk`, `isVtt`, `cues` say why; a cross-origin VTT
   fetches fine yet never loads while `crossorigin` is `null` on the `<video>`). Compare `covered`
   with `duration`, look at the frame at any `maxGap` over 60 s, and take `kind` `subtitles` or
   `speakerOrSound` false while frames show several speakers or sound effects as dialogue-only.
   A passing file names the speaker and the sound, as in the Understanding's two examples —
   "(music) / Using rope to tie knots was an important skill" and "[Orchestral Suite No. 3 in D
   major, BWV 1068, Air] / ♪ Calm melody with a slow tempo ♪". Compare `sample` with on-screen text
   or a transcript: a mismatch in names, numbers or wording is
   [F8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F8), and so is a cue that reverses the
   meaning (f51b46 Failed Example 2 flips mouse and computer). Compare the language of `sample`,
   `srclang` and `label` with the page `lang` and the frames' text. Read the three frames once more
   for Note 4, "Captions should not obscure or obstruct relevant information in the video": captions
   sitting over burned-in subtitles, a speaker's name card, a chart or the slide text.
   Done when every captioned player has its numbers plus a defect, a pass or a `cannot-tell`.

5. Test the exception for each player without captions (ACT ab4d13;
   [F74](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F74),
   [F75](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F75)). Expectation 2 first: "Each target
   element is labeled as a video alternative for text on the page by content that is visible and
   included in the accessibility tree" — the snapshot or `textAlternativeNearby` holds text such as
   "watch the same information again in video form". Expectation 1: "All the information contained
   in each test target is available as text that is visible and included in the accessibility
   tree", including the on-screen text of the step 3 frames.

   ```bash
   agent-browser is visible "<label css>"
   agent-browser snapshot -s "<label css>"
   ```

   No label: F74 (ab4d13 Failed Example 3). A label that `is visible` reports false, or that lives
   only in `aria-describedby`, `title` or visually hidden text: still F74 — ab4d13 Failed Example 4
   and f51b46 Failed Example 4 both fail on an association that is not visible text, and Failed
   Example 3 of f51b46 shows that a paragraph merely describing the audio is not captions either.
   Text that `is visible` reports false: ab4d13 Failed Example 2. Label present, but the video shows
   steps, names or figures the text lacks: F75 (ab4d13 Failed Example 1). Label and plausibly
   complete text: queue "does the video say more than the text?".
   Done when every uncaptioned player is marked labeled, unlabeled or no-alternative.

6. Record evidence: `highlight "<selector>"` then `screenshot --full`, a step 3 frame for missing
   open captions, the player menu shot for an embed, the frame that shows captions covering
   on-screen text, and 10 s of playback (`record start`, `record stop`) for a track that never
   displays. A player that cannot be opened (blocked embed, login wall, DRM) is `cannot-tell` with
   the embed URL and the blocker. Done when every fail has an evidence path and every player in the
   findings file has an outcome.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): a visible `<video>` marked audio yes with no `captions` or `subtitles`
  track (or only `descriptions`, `chapters` or `metadata` tracks, or a caption track with
  `trackReadyState` 3 or `loadedCues` 0), no caption text in the three frames taken with the
  player's captions control switched on (or no such control in the snapshot) and no visible label
  naming it an alternative for text (95). An embedded player with no captions control and no
  captions entry in its menu, and no label on the page (90). A player whose only text is a
  transcript or a paragraph describing the audio, with nothing labelling the video an alternative
  for that text (90; f51b46 Failed Examples 3 and 4).
- Fail (70–89): only auto-generated captions and a frame shows a wrong word, a missed speaker
  change or a missing sound description (80); a human-authored cue that contradicts the speech or
  the on-screen text (80; f51b46 Failed Example 2); `covered` under a third of `duration`, or
  `maxGap` over 60 s while the frame shows someone speaking (80); page text or a transcript exists
  but no visible text labels the video as its alternative, F74 (80); a label that exists only in
  `aria-describedby`, `title`, hidden or off-screen text, F74 (85; ab4d13 Failed Example 4); the
  labeled text lacks what the video shows on screen, F75 (75); a `subtitles` track with
  `speakerOrSound` false (70); captions that hide burned-in subtitles, a name card, a chart or
  slide text in the frames, captions Note 4 (75).
- Needs confirmation (50–69; record `cannot-tell` with the certainty and the question): only
  auto-generated captions with no error observed (60): ask the human to watch 60 s and note errors,
  missing speaker names and sound cues; a human-authored track with `covered` between a third and
  half of `duration` or `speakerOrSound` false (55): the same question; `srclang` or the `sample`
  language differs from both the page `lang` and the frames' on-screen text (60): ask which language
  the speech is in; a labeled alternative whose text seems complete (60): ask whether the video says
  more; a scripted player with neither `<video>` nor a captions control (55): ask whether it has
  captions at all; a SMIL presentation no browser plays (55): ask whether the caption region shows.
- Pass (record `pass`, no queue entry): every visible player with audio has a human-authored track
  with `trackReadyState` 2, `covered` ≥ half of `duration`, `maxGap` ≤ 60 s and `speakerOrSound`
  true, open captions in all three frames, or a human-authored captions entry in the player; and no
  page state reveals another player.
- Not present: no state of the sample contains a visible video with audio.
- False positives to avoid: a `<video>` without an audio track (background loop, `audioBytes` 0)
  and `<audio>` are 1.2.1; a `<video>` used only for its poster; a scripted player whose captions
  come from its own CC button instead of a `<track>`; a `subtitles` track that does name speakers
  and sounds is a caption track (Note 5: "In some countries, captions are called subtitles");
  captions off by default are fine (Note 2: closed captions "can be turned on and off with some
  players"); an audio description track left uncaptioned (Note 6: audio descriptions "do not need
  to be captioned"); caption wording, line length or placement that differs from a house style, on
  the Understanding's note that "Style guides for captions may differ among different languages"; a
  live stream is 1.2.4.

## Reporting

- File under: 1.2.2. A missing audio description on the same video is a separate 1.2.3 (or 1.2.5)
  issue; silent video or `<audio>` without an alternative is 1.2.1; a live stream is 1.2.4. One
  issue per video with a distinct defect; the same defect on several samples is one issue.
- Title patterns: "Video has no captions", "Captions cover only part of the video", "Caption track
  does not load", "Only auto-generated captions available", "Captions do not name the speakers or
  the sounds", "Captions cover text in the picture", "Video alternative to text unlabeled".
- Severity: High when the video carries information found nowhere else on the page or is the
  sample's main content, and High for a video in a process the visitor must complete (a form
  explainer, an onboarding step); Medium when the page text covers most of it but the label is
  missing, or the captions have gaps or errors that leave the story followable; Low when the audio
  is incidental (background music, a jingle) and nothing spoken is lost.
- Type: Content (writing or uploading the caption file, correcting cues, adding the label);
  Technical when the `<track>` is missing or broken, the file is not WebVTT, `crossorigin` is
  absent on a cross-origin track or the player lacks CC; Design when the only defect is captions
  obscuring the picture.
- Evidence: the player with `highlight`, plus a frame mid-playback without caption text or the
  player's captions menu (none or auto-generated); the frame itself when the captions obscure
  on-screen text; a short recording when a track never displays. Name the video, its running time
  and the timestamp of the frame, so the fix can be checked at the same point.
- Recommendation: add a human-authored caption track with dialogue, speaker identification and
  meaningful sounds ([H95](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H95)):

  ```html
  <video src="talk.mp4" controls crossorigin="anonymous">
    <track kind="captions" srclang="en" label="English" src="talk.en.vtt" />
  </video>
  ```

  ```text
  WEBVTT

  00:00:00.000 --> 00:00:02.000
  (music)

  00:00:02.000 --> 00:00:06.000
  NARRATOR: Using rope to tie knots was an important skill
  ```

  `srclang` is the language spoken in the cues; `crossorigin` is required when the VTT is served
  from another origin. On a hosting platform upload the same file as closed captions
  ([G87](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G87)) — or use any readily available
  media format whose player supports closed captioning — or burn the captions into the picture
  ([G93](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G93)); in a SMIL presentation add the
  caption region and its text stream
  ([SM11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/smil/SM11),
  [SM12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/smil/SM12)). A video that only repeats the page
  text needs visible text naming it that alternative instead.

- Related: 1.2.1 (silent video, audio-only), 1.2.3 and 1.2.5 (audio description: separate issue),
  1.2.4 (live), 1.2.8 (AAA; F74 fails both), 1.4.2 (autoplaying sound), 2.1.1 and 4.1.2 (player
  controls, including the captions button), 1.1.1 (the player's own icon buttons). Split by what
  the viewer loses: no captions at all is 1.2.2; the picture undescribed is 1.2.3; an unreachable
  or unnamed CC button is 2.1.1 or 4.1.2 on top of the 1.2.2 issue.
