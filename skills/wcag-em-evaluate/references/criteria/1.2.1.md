## Requirement

> For prerecorded audio-only and prerecorded video-only media, the following are true, except when
> the audio or video is a media alternative for text and is clearly labeled as such:
>
> - Prerecorded Audio-only: An alternative for time-based media is provided that presents
>   equivalent information for prerecorded audio-only content.
>
> - Prerecorded Video-only: Either an alternative for time-based media or an audio track is
>   provided that presents equivalent information for prerecorded video-only content.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary): **prerecorded**: "information that is not live". **live** (Understanding,
Key Terms): "information captured from a real-world event and transmitted to the receiver with no
more than a broadcast delay", Note 2: "If information is completely computer generated, it is not
live." **audio-only**: "a time-based presentation that contains only audio (no video and no
interaction)". **video-only**: "a time-based presentation that contains only video (no audio and no
interaction)". **media alternative for text**: "media that presents no more information than is
already presented in text (directly or via text alternatives)", Note: "Media alternatives for text
may be audio-only, video-only (including sign-language video), or audio-video." **alternative for
time-based media**: "document including correctly sequenced text descriptions of time-based visual
and auditory information and providing a means for achieving the outcomes of any time-based
interaction", Note: "A screenplay used to create the synchronized media content would meet this
definition only if it was corrected to accurately represent the final synchronized media after
editing."

## In plain terms

Audio without picture (a podcast, a recorded speech, a voice message) needs a text transcript; video
without sound (a silent screen recording, a how-to animation, a muted product demo) needs a text
description or an audio track that narrates what is shown. The alternative carries the same
information in the same order — speech and who is speaking, meaningful sounds, everything that
happens on screen — because text can be rendered as speech, as large print or as braille, so people
who are deaf, hard of hearing, deaf-blind or blind lose nothing. An audio track for a silent video
additionally lets sighted and blind users take the content in at the same time. Media that only
repeats text already on the page is exempt, provided the page says so in words the reader can see.

## Applies when

- Present: a prerecorded presentation with one channel only that the user can start. Audio-only: an
  `<audio>` with `autoplay`, or with a play button that is visible and in the accessibility tree
  (native `controls`, or a scripted button); a script-built or embedded audio player (`provider`
  `soundcloud`, `spotify`, `other-player`); a link to an audio file (`.mp3`, `.m4a`, `.wav`,
  `.ogg`). Video-only: a visible `<video>` with no audio track, one that autoplays muted without
  controls, an embedded player the page presents as silent, a link to a silent video file, or an
  animation that conveys a sequence. Detect with `media.js` (`media[]` with `kind`, `autoplay`,
  `playing`, `muted`, `controls`, `duration`, `tracks`, `audioTracks`, `poster`,
  `textAlternativeNearby`; `animatedImages[]`), `motion.js` (`animations[]`, `carousels[]` catch a
  canvas, CSS or script animation that the gif/apng/webp filter of `animatedImages[]` misses),
  `structure.js` (`iframes[].src`, for players whose `provider` comes back null) and `links.js`
  (`links[].href`).
- Not present: none of the above in any state of the sample. A `<video>` with sound is synchronized
  media (1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.5) and does not make this criterion applicable. Live audio-only is 1.2.9;
  live media with audio is 1.2.4. Three shapes are outside every ACT rule here and are recorded as
  inapplicable, not as a fail: a `<video>` that is not visible (ee13b5, d7ba54, fd26cf, c3232f
  Inapplicable Example 2); an `<audio>` with no controls, or with controls that are hidden, that
  does not autoplay (2eb176 Inapplicable Examples 1 and 2); and a `<video>` used only to show its
  `poster`, because the shared ACT assumption "A mechanism is available to start the video and the
  video element is not simply used to display the poster" does not hold — judge that poster under
  1.1.1 instead.
- Exceptions:
  - The normative one, "except when the audio or video is a media alternative for text and is
    clearly labeled as such". Both halves must hold, and both must be visible and in the
    accessibility tree. ACT afb423 Expectation 1: "The auditory information of each test target is
    available as text (directly or via text alternatives) that is visible and included in the
    accessibility tree." Expectation 2: "Each target element is labeled as an audio alternative for
    text on the page by content that is visible and included in the accessibility tree." ACT fd26cf
    states the same two for video-only, with "video alternative for text" in Expectation 2.
  - Understanding, Intent note, verbatim: "A text equivalent is not required for audio that is
    provided as an equivalent for video with no audio information. For example, it is not required
    to caption audio description that is provided as an alternative to a silent movie." An `<audio>`
    that narrates a neighbouring silent video therefore needs no transcript of its own.
  - Not prerecorded: a live stream, "information captured from a real-world event and transmitted to
    the receiver with no more than a broadcast delay". Recognise it by `media[].duration` coming
    back `null`, a "LIVE" badge in the player, or a manifest source (`.m3u8`, `.mpd`). Completely
    computer-generated output is never live (glossary Note 2), so a synthesised voice clip is
    prerecorded and in scope.
  - Understanding, example "An animation that illustrates how a car engine works": where the
    surrounding text already gives the full explanation, "the media is an alternative for text and
    the text alternative includes only a brief description of the animation and refers to the
    tutorial text for more information". A brief description plus a pointer to that text passes; do
    not fail such an animation for having a short description.

## axe-core

| Rule            | Decides                         | Result → outcome                       |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| `audio-caption` | flags each `<audio>` for review | incomplete → step 4; never a violation |

`audio-caption` sits in the deprecated rule set: off by default, not run by `agent-browser a11y`,
and `needs review` only, so no result for it appears in `.notes/audit/axe/` and nothing here waits
on it. It also asks for captions, which is not what this criterion asks of audio-only content: an
`<audio>` that has a `<track kind="captions">` still fails 1.2.1 when no transcript exists. Its ACT
mapping, 2eb176 and afb423, is exactly what steps 4 and 5 run by hand.

Settles: nothing. Leaves open: finding the media in every state, filtering it by the ACT
applicability, telling silent video from video with sound, locating the transcript, description or
audio track, judging it equivalent and correctly sequenced, confirming it is visible and in the
accessibility tree, and recognising the labelled media-alternative exception.

## Manual procedure

Walk the default state first, then repeat steps 1 to 3 in every state that reveals a player: consent
wall accepted (`reload` after), dialog open, accordion, tab or carousel slide shown. For a player
inside an iframe, work in the frame and come back:

```bash
agent-browser frame "iframe[src*='youtube']"
agent-browser snapshot -i
agent-browser frame main
```

1. Inventory the media. Run the probes and keep every candidate with its selector, kind and state:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/media.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/motion.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/structure.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/links.js
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   ```

   Keep every `media[]` item with its `kind`, `sources`, `provider`, `autoplay`, `playing`, `muted`,
   `controls`, `duration`, `tracks`, `audioTracks`, `poster` and `textAlternativeNearby`; every
   `animatedImages[]` entry and every `motion.js` `animations[]` or `carousels[]` entry that runs
   longer than a hover effect; every `structure.js` `iframes[].src` on an audio or video host that
   `media.js` did not classify; and every `links.js` `links[].href` ending in an audio or video
   extension. Done when every candidate is recorded together with the state it appeared in.

2. Filter the inventory by the ACT applicability before testing anything. Read the raw element
   state:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (() => [...document.querySelectorAll('audio, video')].map((el) => ({
     tag: el.localName,
     src: el.currentSrc || el.getAttribute('src') || null,
     sourceEls: el.querySelectorAll('source').length,
     controls: el.controls,
     autoplay: el.hasAttribute('autoplay'),
     playing: !el.paused && !el.ended,
     noSource: el.networkState === el.NETWORK_NO_SOURCE,
     nothingLoaded: el.readyState === el.HAVE_NOTHING,
     poster: el.getAttribute('poster') || null,
     visible: el.getClientRects().length > 0 && getComputedStyle(el).visibility !== 'hidden'
   })))()
   JS
   ```

   `<audio>` is applicable when ACT e7aa44 applies: it "has an autoplay attribute value of true; or,
   has a play button that is visible and included in the accessibility tree" — `controls: true` with
   `visible: true`, or a scripted play button that `snapshot -i` shows with a play-like accessible
   name. `controls: false` and `autoplay: false` with no such button is a sound effect, inapplicable
   (2eb176 Inapplicable Example 1); `controls: true` with `visible: false` is inapplicable to 2eb176
   and afb423 (Inapplicable Example 2) yet still applicable to e7aa44 when `autoplay` is true, so
   keep an autoplaying hidden `<audio>`. `<video>` is applicable when it is visible: drop
   `visible: false`. Drop any element whose `noSource` is true, or whose `nothingLoaded` is true
   with a `poster` and no `sourceEls`: it only paints its poster. Drop live sources. Done when each
   candidate reads `applicable` or `inapplicable`, with the ACT rule that decided it.

3. Classify each remaining `<video>` as silent or with sound. The script measures, then puts every
   player back the way it found it:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'JS'
   (async () => { const out = [];
     for (const v of document.querySelectorAll('video')) {
       const wasMuted = v.muted, wasPaused = v.paused, wasTime = v.currentTime;
       v.muted = true; try { await v.play(); } catch (e) {}
       await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 2000)); v.pause();
       const dec = v.webkitVideoDecodedByteCount ?? 0, b = v.webkitAudioDecodedByteCount ?? null;
       const played = v.readyState >= v.HAVE_CURRENT_DATA && v.currentTime > 0 && dec > 0;
       v.currentTime = wasTime; v.muted = wasMuted;
       if (!wasPaused) v.play().catch(() => {});
       out.push({ src: v.currentSrc || null, controls: v.controls, played,
         live: v.duration === Infinity,
         duration: Number.isFinite(v.duration) ? Math.round(v.duration) : String(v.duration),
         audioTracks: v.audioTracks ? v.audioTracks.length : null,
         hasAudio: v.mozHasAudio ?? (v.audioTracks?.length > 0 ? true
           : !played || b == null ? null : b > 0) });
     }
     return out; })()
   JS
   ```

   `hasAudio: false` with `played: true` → video-only, step 6. `muted` and `autoplay` true with
   `controls` false → step 7. `hasAudio: true` with controls → synchronized media (ACT f51b46,
   1a02b0): hand it to the 1.2.2, 1.2.3 and 1.2.5 files. f51b46 applies only where "the video
   contains audio that is not only silence", so an `audioTracks` count above zero on a clip the
   player shows as silent stays a video-only candidate. `played: false` → `hasAudio` is null (the
   byte counter moves only after real playback): `click` the visible play control, re-run once after
   `wait --load networkidle`, then treat as unmeasured. `hasAudio: null` → video-only candidate;
   record `cannot-tell` unless step 6 finds an alternative. `live: true`, or `duration: "Infinity"`
   → live, drop it to 1.2.4 or 1.2.9. An embed cannot be measured from the page: treat it as video
   with sound unless the page itself says it is silent. Done when every video is classified.

4. Audio-only: locate the alternative for time-based media (ACT 2eb176, technique
   [G158](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G158)), in order: `textAlternativeNearby`
   non-null; a heading, text block or disclosure by the player whose text starts with "Transcript",
   "Transcriptie" or the page-language equivalent; a link in the same section whose text or `name`
   contains "transcript". Run the block that matches what you found:

   ```bash
   # inline: confirm both halves of the 2eb176 expectation
   agent-browser is visible "<transcript css>"
   agent-browser snapshot -s "<transcript css>"
   ```

   ```bash
   # disclosure: reveal, then re-read the revealed text
   agent-browser find text "Transcript" click
   agent-browser snapshot -s "<transcript css>"
   ```

   ```bash
   # link: read its target, then return and refresh the refs
   agent-browser get attr "<transcript link css>" href
   agent-browser open <transcript href>
   agent-browser read
   agent-browser back
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   ```

   ACT 2eb176 expects: "The auditory information of each test target is available through a text
   transcript. That text transcript is visible and included in the accessibility tree, either on the
   page or through a link." So an inline transcript must satisfy both `is visible` and
   `snapshot -s`; a linked one only has to be reachable. Check a linked transcript with
   `curl -sIL <transcript href> | grep -i '^HTTP'`: a final 4xx or 5xx is "none" (dead link); a 200
   whose body `read` cannot render (PDF, DOCX) is a transcript the agent cannot read — record
   `cannot-tell` with the URL for the human to open. Done when each audio item carries a transcript
   location (inline, disclosed, linked), "none" or `cannot-tell`.

5. Audio-only: judge the transcript, or the exception, from the text (the agent cannot listen). The
   yardstick is Understanding's press-conference example: "The transcript includes a verbatim record
   of everything the speakers say. It identifies who is speaking as well as noting other significant
   sounds that are part of the recording, such as applause, laughter, questions from the audience,
   and so on." Fail signals: a filename, a placeholder ("Transcript coming soon") or the title
   repeated ([F30](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F30)); a summary, teaser or
   show notes, or a transcript far shorter than the speech the duration and context suggest,
   allowing that music or sparse speech legitimately yields a short one
   ([F67](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F67)); a transcript about other content
   — its heading, `<title>` or subject names something the clip is not, the way 2eb176 Failed
   Example 3 links a "moon speech" audio to a transcript that says "cheese"; the screenplay the
   recording was made from, still carrying directions and lines that were cut (glossary Note: it
   counts "only if it was corrected to accurately represent the final synchronized media after
   editing"); no speaker identification in an interview; a transcript that is `display: none`,
   `aria-hidden="true"` or absent from `snapshot -s` (2eb176 Failed Example 5). With no transcript,
   test the exception (ACT afb423): visible text by the player says the audio is a spoken version of
   the page text ("Listen to this article"), that label is itself in the accessibility tree
   (afb423 Failed Example 4), and the text it points at is the full article rather than an intro
   (Failed Example 1) and is visible (Failed Example 2). Done when each audio item is `pass`, a fail
   indicator with a certainty, or `cannot-tell` plus a note of what a human has to listen to.

6. Video-only: locate the alternative (ACT c3232f, which passes only if one of ee13b5, d7ba54 or
   fd26cf passes). In order:
   - A text description on the page or one link away that walks through what happens on screen in
     sequence, including on-screen text and, for a screen recording, the steps performed
     ([G159](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G159)). ee13b5 and 1a02b0 expect it
     "available through a text transcript that is included in the accessibility tree, and is either
     on the page or linked" — note what that leaves out: unlike the audio rule, it does not have to
     be visible. Confirm it with `agent-browser snapshot -s "<description css>"` and accept one the
     tree exposes even when it is positioned off screen (ee13b5 Passed Example 3); reject one the
     tree does not expose (Failed Example 3).
   - A described audio track presented as such: a sibling `<audio controls>` next to the video, a
     "listen to the described version" link, or a second video labelled "with audio description"
     ([G166](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G166), ACT d7ba54, whose Passed
     Example 1 is exactly a silent `<video>` followed by an `<audio controls>` description).
   - The labelled exception as in step 5, read against fd26cf's two expectations.

   A `<track kind="descriptions">` on its own does not count. c3232f states it: "there is no native
   support in any major browser for this technique. Technique H96: Using the track element to
   provide audio descriptions can not be relied upon to conform to 1.2.1" — so record
   `tracks[].kind` as context only and cite
   [H96](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H96) as advisory (c3232f Failed Example 4).
   Apply step 5's F30, F67, wrong-subject and screenplay signals to the description as well. Done
   when each video-only item is `pass`, a fail indicator with a certainty, or `cannot-tell`.

7. Animated images and muted background loops (`animatedImages[]`, `motion.js` `animations[]`,
   `autoplay` plus `muted` without `controls`): read the alt text, the nearby text and the context.
   Motion that only sets a mood (a hero loop, a parallax texture, a spinner): record "decorative"
   and why. Motion that shows a sequence a reader needs (a how-to GIF, a chart building up, a
   product demo): back to step 6. Motion whose surrounding text already explains it in full is the
   car-engine case from "Applies when": a brief description that refers to that text passes. Done
   when each such item is marked decorative, judged under step 6, or recorded as the car-engine
   exception with the text it points at.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): an applicable `<audio>` (autoplay, or a visible play button in the
  accessibility tree) with a finite duration, no transcript inline, disclosed or linked in any
  state, and no visible text labelling it as an audio version of page text (ACT e7aa44 failed,
  2eb176 Failed Example 1) — 92. A visible `<video>` measured `played: true` and `hasAudio: false`,
  or presented as silent, with no description, no described audio track and no media-alternative
  label (ACT c3232f failed, d7ba54 Failed Example 1) — 90. A transcript or description link whose
  final response is 4xx or 5xx — 90.
- Fail (70–89): the transcript is a summary, show notes or far shorter than the duration allows
  ([F67](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F67)) — 80; a placeholder, a filename or
  the title repeated ([F30](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F30)) — 85. A
  transcript or description whose heading, `<title>` or subject is about other content than the clip
  (2eb176 Failed Examples 2 to 4, ee13b5 Failed Examples 1 and 2, c3232f Failed Example 1) — 80. A
  screenplay or shooting script offered as the transcript, with directions or cut lines still in it
  — 75. An audio-only transcript hidden visually or from the accessibility tree — 85 (ACT 2eb176
  Failed Example 5). A video-only description absent from the accessibility tree — 85 (ACT ee13b5
  Failed Example 3); a visually hidden description that is in the tree passes (ee13b5 Passed Example
  3). A silent video relying only on `<track kind="descriptions">` — 80 (c3232f Failed Example 4). A
  separate `<audio>` offered as the described version that is about other content, or whose source
  is the video's own soundtrack — 75 (d7ba54 Failed Example 2). A muted autoplaying video without
  controls showing a demo, an instruction or on-screen text, with no alternative — 85. A
  media-alternative claim whose page text is not visible (`display: none`, off screen) — 85 (afb423
  Failed Example 2, fd26cf Failed Example 2); whose label is present but not visible — 85 (afb423
  Failed Example 4, fd26cf Failed Example 4); with equivalent text on the page but nothing labelling
  the media as an alternative to it — 80 (afb423 Failed Example 3, fd26cf Failed Example 3); over
  page text visibly narrower than the media — 75 (afb423 Failed Example 1). An interview transcript
  with no speaker identification — 70. An embedded audio player with no transcript on the page or in
  its frame — 78.
- Needs confirmation (50–69): a `<video>` whose audio could not be measured (`played: false` on the
  second run, or `hasAudio: null`), with no alternative — 60; ask whether it has sound. A `<video>`
  whose only evidence of sound is a non-zero `audioTracks` count on a clip the page presents as
  silent — 60; ask whether that track is anything but silence (ACT f51b46 applies only to audio
  "that is not only silence"). A muted looping background video with no alternative — 55; ask
  whether it shows anything a reader needs. An animated image conveying a sequence with only short
  alt text — 55; ask whether the motion carries information beyond the alt text and the text around
  it. An audio-version label over partial page text — 55; ask what the audio adds.
- Pass: every applicable audio-only item has a transcript that is visible and in the accessibility
  tree, on the page or one link away, of plausible length, identifying the speakers and the
  significant sounds, or is a labelled media alternative for the full visible page text (afb423,
  both expectations). Every applicable video-only item has a text description in the accessibility
  tree, on the page or linked (visible or not), a described audio track presented as such, or the
  labelled exception (fd26cf, both expectations). A plausible transcript passes; note it for a human
  to spot-check against the audio.
- Not present: no prerecorded audio-only or video-only media in any state of the sample.
- False positives to avoid: a `<video>` with sound belongs to 1.2.2, 1.2.3 and 1.2.5; live streams
  to 1.2.4 and 1.2.9. An `<audio>` with `controls` and `autoplay` both false is a sound effect, and
  a `<video>` that only paints its `poster` is a still image (1.1.1). A described audio track and a
  labelled media alternative for text need no transcript, and neither does an audio track that is
  itself the description of a silent video (Understanding note). A video-only description that is
  visually hidden but in the accessibility tree passes. A sign-language video may be the media
  alternative for text, not a failure of it (glossary Note). An animation inside a tutorial whose
  text already explains it in full needs only a brief description that refers to that text. A
  transcript one link away or revealed by a disclosure passes (ACT 2eb176); one served as PDF or
  DOCX is `cannot-tell`, not a fail.

## Reporting

- File under: 1.2.1 for a missing, wrong, incomplete or hidden transcript, description or audio
  track on audio-only or video-only media, and for a media-alternative claim that is unlabelled or
  unsupported by the page text. A video with sound lacking captions is 1.2.2, lacking audio
  description 1.2.3 or 1.2.5; a live stream is 1.2.4 or 1.2.9; an unnamed player button or a player
  whose state is not exposed is 4.1.2; a player the keyboard cannot operate is 2.1.1; a poster or
  clip that is not identified at all is 1.1.1. One issue per player or clip; a clip that repeats on
  other samples is one issue naming those samples.
- Title patterns: "Podcast episode has no transcript", "Silent product video has no text
  description", "Transcript of the interview is only a summary", "Transcript link leads nowhere",
  "Transcript belongs to a different recording", "Video is offered as an alternative to the page
  text without saying so", "Description track is not played by any browser"
- Severity: High when the media is the only carrier of its information — main content, an
  instruction, a product demo, a recorded announcement — so deaf, hard-of-hearing and deaf-blind
  readers get nothing from audio-only content and blind and low-vision readers get nothing from
  video-only content; High too when the alternative exists but is unreachable (dead link, hidden
  from the tree). Medium when the page text already carries most of it, when the transcript is
  present but incomplete, or when every clip across the product has the same gap. Low when a
  complete, correct alternative exists and is merely hard to find or placed far from the player.
- Type: Content when a transcript or description has to be written, corrected or completed, or when
  the media-alternative label has to be worded. Technical when the alternative exists and the markup
  keeps it from users: `aria-hidden` or `display: none` on the transcript, a broken link, a
  `<track kind="descriptions">` nothing plays, a player that hides its own controls. Design does not
  apply.
- Evidence: `highlight "<player css>"` then a page `screenshot` showing the player together with the
  text around it, so the absence of a transcript is visible in one frame. For a transcript that is
  the wrong content or only a summary, add `screenshot "<transcript css>" <path>` so the two can be
  compared. For a dead link, screenshot the error page the link lands on. For a player inside an
  iframe, `frame` into it first and screenshot there, then `frame main`. For a muted autoplaying
  loop whose defect is the motion itself, record a short GIF.
- Recommendation: audio-only — publish a full transcript under the player, or link it immediately
  after, identifying the speakers and the significant sounds
  ([G158](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G158)):

  ```html
  <audio controls src="/media/chairmans-speech.mp3"></audio>
  <p><a href="/media/chairmans-speech-transcript.html">Transcript of the chairman's speech</a></p>
  ```

  Video-only — give a text description of the visual content in the order it happens
  ([G159](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G159)), or an audio track that describes
  it, presented as such ([G166](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G166)):

  ```html
  <video controls src="/media/engine.mp4"></video>
  <h2>What the engine animation shows</h2>
  <p>The piston moves down and draws the fuel-air mixture into the cylinder, ...</p>
  ```
