# 2.4.6 Headings and Labels

Level AA · WCAG 2.0+ · Guideline 2.4 Navigable · Group: `structure`

Sources: [Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/headings-and-labels.html) ·
[Spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#headings-and-labels) ·
[Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/#headings-and-labels) ·
[Plain English](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/2-4-6-headings-and-labels/) ·
ACT: [cc0f0a](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/cc0f0a/),
[b49b2e](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/b49b2e/)

## Requirement

> Headings and labels describe topic or purpose.

Terms (WCAG 2.2 glossary), verbatim; the first decides what counts as a label here, the second
decides what belongs to 4.1.2 instead:

- **label**: "text or other component with a text alternative that is presented to a user to
  identify a component within web content". Note 1: "A label is presented to all users whereas the
  name may be hidden and only exposed by assistive technology. In many (but not all) cases the name
  and the label are the same." Note 2: "The term label is not limited to the label element in
  HTML." Note 2 is the clause that puts a button's text, a tab, a menu item, a `legend` and a
  visible placeholder in scope; Note 1 is the clause that keeps a name exposed only to assistive
  technology out of it.
- **name**: "text by which software can identify a component within web content to the user". Note
  1: "The name may be hidden and only exposed by assistive technology, whereas a label is presented
  to all users. In many (but not all) cases, the label and the name are the same." An `aria-label`
  on a control with no visible text is a name and not a label: it is judged under 4.1.2, never
  here.

ACT glossary, the terms the two rules' expectations turn on:

- **programmatic label** (cc0f0a): "Element L is a programmatic label of target element T if
  either: T is a labeled control of L; or L is referenced by ID in the aria-labelledby attribute of
  T." Note: "a given element may have more than one programmatic label." Each programmatic label is
  its own test target with its own verdict, so a field with two of them gets two (step 4).
- **visual context** (cc0f0a): "The visual context of a node is everything that is visually or
  logically located near it when the document containing it is rendered. Only visible information
  may be part of the visual context." "The logical distance is the distance in the structure of the
  document: either the tree distance within the DOM tree, or the semantic relation. For example,
  headings are logically near the content that follows (until the next heading of the same or
  higher level) even though they might be far away visually or in the DOM tree." It "may include,
  but is not limited to, headings, text in the same sentence or paragraph". Note: "As a rule of
  thumb, visual context should be close enough to be displayed on the device at the same time as
  the element it relates to." Note: "Visual context that is located before (in reading order) the
  element it relates to is often more useful than visual context located after." Sentence three is
  what lets a repeated "Name" pass under a visible "Shipping"; sentence two is what makes an
  invisible one worthless.
- **visible** (both rules): "Content is considered visible if making it fully transparent would
  result in a difference in the pixels rendered for any part of the document that is currently
  within the viewport or can be brought into the viewport via scrolling." A heading or label at
  `top: -9999px` is not visible and is not visual context; one far below the fold is visible,
  because scrolling brings it in.
- **perceivable content** (b49b2e), the content each heading is judged against: the node "is
  palpable content", "is either visible or included in the accessibility tree", and "if the node is
  an element, it does not have a semantic role of none or presentation".
- **marked as decorative** (b49b2e), what that judgement skips over: "it has an explicit role of
  none or presentation; or it is an img element with an alt attribute whose value is the empty
  string (alt=""), and with no explicit role".

## In plain terms

Every heading tells the reader what the section under it is about, and every label tells the reader
what to enter or what the control does. Short is fine: "Labels and headings do not need to be
lengthy. A word, or even a single character, may suffice if it provides an appropriate cue to
finding and navigating content", and an image serves as a label when the author "ensure[s] that the
image and its use as a label (in context) are widely understood". The criterion judges the wording
of the headings and labels that exist: it "does not require headings or labels" (3.3.2 does, for
inputs), nor their markup (1.3.1), nor an accessible name (4.1.2) — but "it does require that if
labels are present, they must be accurate and sufficiently clear or descriptive". It serves people
who move through a page by its heading list, "in an automatically generated list of headings, a
table of contents, or when jumping from heading to heading within a page", people whose reading is
slow or whose short-term memory is limited, and anyone filling in a form. Voice-control and
keyboard users reach a control by speaking or scanning its label, so a label that names the wrong
thing costs them the control.

## Applies when

- Present: a heading in the accessibility tree with a non-empty name (a `structure.js` `headings`
  entry that also appears in `snapshot`: `h1` to `h6` or `role="heading"`), text styled as a
  heading without markup (`visuallyStyledHeadings`), a visible field label (`forms.js` field with
  `visibleLabelText`, an `aria-labelledby` name or `legend`, or a `placeholder` as its only
  visible text), or a button, tab, menu item or switch with visible text or an icon with a text
  alternative (`snapshot -i`; `images.js` entry with `inButton: true`), in the sample or any
  recorded state (a dialog's heading, an error summary, a wizard step).
- Not present: no heading (in the tree or styled), no visible field label and no labelled control
  in the sample or any recorded state: a page of running text and links only (links are 2.4.4).
- Exceptions: none in the criterion text. Scope limits from the Understanding: the absence of a
  heading or label is "covered separately by 3.3.2"; whether one is "correctly marked up or
  identified" is 1.3.1, and content can "pass this success criterion [...] while failing Success
  Criterion 1.3.1", or pass 1.3.1 "while failing this success criterion"; a name given another way
  (`aria-label`) is 4.1.2, and a control can "pass Success Criterion 4.1.2, but [...] still fail
  this success criterion"; a visible label left out of the name is 2.5.3 — "Having a label which is
  not included in the accessible name is a violation of Success Criterion 2.5.3: Label in Name but
  not of this rule nor of Success Criterion 2.4.6" (cc0f0a Background).
- Applicability of ACT b49b2e, which fixes which headings get an outcome: "any semantic heading
  element that is included in the accessibility tree and has a non-empty ("") accessible name". A
  heading programmatically hidden (`hidden`, `display: none`, `visibility: hidden`, inside
  `aria-hidden="true"`) is out; an off-screen heading that is still in the tree is in.
- Applicability of ACT cc0f0a, two conditions that must both hold: the element has one of the
  semantic roles "checkbox, combobox, listbox, menuitemcheckbox, menuitemradio, radio, searchbox,
  slider, spinbutton, switch, textbox", or "is an input with no semantic role and a type attribute
  value of color, date, datetime-local, file, month, password, time, or week"; and, separately,
  "both the element and the programmatic label must be visible". A visible field whose only
  programmatic label is off screen has no ACT outcome and goes to 3.3.2 (its Inapplicable Example
  2); an invisible field is out whatever its label (its Inapplicable Example 3).
- Outcome mapping both rules publish, which fixes how far a pass carries: "Any failed outcomes:
  success criterion is not satisfied"; "All passed outcomes: success criterion needs further
  testing"; "An inapplicable outcome: success criterion needs further testing". Passing both rules
  is therefore never a `pass` on its own — buttons, tabs, menu items, icon labels, placeholder-only
  labels and headings that exist only in styling are outside both rules and are decided by steps 1,
  2 and 5.

## Manual procedure

Structure mode: read the sample as loaded, then each recorded state that adds headings or labelled
controls (a dialog, an expanded section, a wizard step, the error state). `snapshot` gives the
level and name of every heading in the tree and the name of every field and button; wording the
screen shows but the name leaves out, or the reverse, comes from the probes and the screenshot.
Meaning is the evaluator's reading; `cannot-tell` is for content the evaluator cannot read.

Write one line per judged element into `.notes/audit/findings/<sample>--structure.md`, in this exact
shape, so that step 7 can grep it across samples:

```text
2.4.6 | heading|label|control | "<text>" | <selector> | describes|does-not-describe|cannot-tell
```

1. Inventory the headings and the content each one introduces:

   ```bash
   agent-browser wait --load networkidle
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/structure.js
   agent-browser snapshot
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'EOF'
   (() => {
     const css = (e) => {
       const p = [];
       let n = e;
       while (n && n.nodeType === 1 && n !== document.documentElement) {
         if (n.id) { p.unshift('#' + CSS.escape(n.id)); break; }
         const t = n.tagName.toLowerCase();
         const sib = n.parentElement
           ? [...n.parentElement.children].filter((c) => c.tagName === n.tagName)
           : [];
         p.unshift(sib.length > 1 ? t + ':nth-of-type(' + (sib.indexOf(n) + 1) + ')' : t);
         n = n.parentElement;
       }
       return p.join(' > ');
     };
     const roleAttr = (e) => (e.getAttribute('role') || '').trim().split(/\s+/)[0].toLowerCase();
     const hidden = (e) => {
       if (getComputedStyle(e).visibility !== 'visible') return true;
       for (let n = e; n; n = n.parentElement) {
         if (getComputedStyle(n).display === 'none') return true;
         if (n.getAttribute('aria-hidden') === 'true') return true;
       }
       return false;
     };
     const decorative = (e) => ['none', 'presentation'].includes(roleAttr(e))
       || (e.tagName === 'IMG' && e.getAttribute('alt') === '' && !e.hasAttribute('role'));
     const level = (e) => {
       const r = roleAttr(e);
       if (r === 'heading') return Number(e.getAttribute('aria-level')) || 2;
       if (r) return null;
       const m = /^H([1-6])$/.exec(e.tagName);
       return m ? Number(m[1]) : null;
     };
     const MEDIA = ['IMG', 'SVG', 'VIDEO', 'AUDIO', 'CANVAS', 'INPUT', 'BUTTON', 'SELECT',
       'TEXTAREA', 'IFRAME', 'OBJECT'];
     const perceivable = (n) => {
       if (n.nodeType === 3) {
         const p = n.parentElement;
         return n.data.trim().length > 0 && !!p && !hidden(p) && !decorative(p);
       }
       return MEDIA.includes(n.tagName.toUpperCase()) && !hidden(n) && !decorative(n);
     };
     const seq = [];
     const SHOW = NodeFilter.SHOW_ELEMENT | NodeFilter.SHOW_TEXT;
     const w = document.createTreeWalker(document.body, SHOW);
     while (w.nextNode()) seq.push(w.currentNode);
     const out = [];
     seq.forEach((n, i) => {
       if (n.nodeType !== 1) return;
       const lv = level(n);
       if (lv === null || hidden(n)) return;
       let first = null;
       for (let j = i + 1; j < seq.length; j++) {
         const m = seq[j];
         if (n.contains(m)) continue;
         if (perceivable(m)) { first = m; break; }
       }
       const r = n.getBoundingClientRect();
       out.push({
         selector: css(n),
         level: lv,
         roleAttr: n.getAttribute('role'),
         text: (n.textContent || '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim().slice(0, 120),
         imgAlt: [...n.querySelectorAll('img[alt]')]
           .map((x) => x.getAttribute('alt')).join(' ') || null,
         offScreen: r.bottom + window.scrollY <= 0 || r.right + window.scrollX <= 0,
         firstKind: first ? (first.nodeType === 3 ? 'text' : first.tagName.toLowerCase()) : null,
         firstContent: first
           ? (first.nodeType === 3 ? first.data : (first.getAttribute('alt') ?? ''))
             .replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim().slice(0, 160)
           : null,
       });
     });
     return out;
   })()
   EOF
   ```

   The script returns one row per heading that b49b2e applies to, already filtered: it drops
   programmatically hidden headings (its Inapplicable Example 2) and pairs each remaining heading
   with `firstContent`, "the first perceivable content after the test target that is not
   decorative", in flat-tree order. `text` empty with `imgAlt` set is a heading named by an image
   (its Passed Example 3) and applies; `offScreen: true` is still applicable, because the heading is
   in the tree (its Passed Example 5) — it only means the heading is not visual context for step 4.
   A `roleAttr` of `presentation` or `none` on an `h1`–`h6` removes the row; confirm against the
   `snapshot` line, because Presentational Roles Conflict Resolution can put the heading back in the
   tree, and the rule warns that then "some semantic heading elements can fail this rule with some
   technology but users of other technologies would not experience any accessibility issue".
   Cross-check every row against its `snapshot` line (`heading "<name>" [level=n]`): the snapshot
   name is the accessible name that decides applicability, the script's `text` is the on-screen
   wording. A heading the snapshot leaves nameless has no harmonised ACT outcome — "This rule does
   not include examples of empty headings because there is currently no harmonized approach for
   testing them" — so note it for 1.3.1 and leave it out here. Add the `visuallyStyledHeadings`
   entries from `structure.js` that introduce the block after them: b49b2e Background states that
   headings "visible but not in the accessibility tree [...] are not tested by this rule but they
   can still fail Success Criterion 2.4.6 Headings and Labels". Done when every heading has a row
   carrying its text, its `firstContent` and its `offScreen` flag.

2. Judge each heading against the content it introduces (b49b2e Expectation: the heading "describes
   the topic or purpose of the first perceivable content after the test target that is not
   decorative. The order of elements is determined by the flat tree"). Expand what is collapsed
   first, then re-run step 1:

   ```bash
   agent-browser click "<the disclosure button>"
   agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>-headings.png --full
   ```

   The screenshot carries what the tree cannot: a heading CSS places away from its tree position,
   and the styled headings of step 1.

   Answer for each heading: does it name the topic or purpose of that content? Yes when a reader who
   sees only the heading, in a heading list, can predict the section — "Opening Hours" over "We are
   open Monday through Friday from 10 to 16" (Passed Example 1, and Passed Example 2 with
   `role="heading"`); "A" over the glossary entries starting with A (Passed Example 4, the rule's
   own note being "Headings do not need to be lengthy. A word, or even a single character, may be
   sufficient"); an `img` heading whose `alt` names the section (Passed Example 3). No when the
   heading names another topic — "Weather" over the opening hours (Failed Example 1, and Failed
   Example 2 with `role="heading"`, Failed Example 3 off screen) — or is a placeholder or template
   token ("Heading", "Lorem ipsum", "Untitled", "{{ title }}"), or is a bare number or ordinal
   ("Section 1", "Part 2") over content with a nameable topic.

   Only `firstContent` decides the ACT outcome: "This heading describes the first perceivable
   content after it (the first p element). The next perceivable content (the second p element) is
   not considered by this rule" (Passed Example 6; Failed Example 4 is the same shape failing). So
   a heading that describes its section as a whole while missing the first paragraph is not a
   deterministic fail: read the whole section from the snapshot and the screenshot before deciding,
   and apply the rule's two assumptions, each of which turns a rule failure into a possible pass.
   First: "Due to positioning, it is possible to render a document in an order that greatly differs
   from the tree order, in which case the content which is visually associated with a heading might
   not be the content following it in tree order and this rule might fail while Success Criterion
   2.4.6 Headings and Labels is still satisfied." Second: "This rule also assumes that the content
   the heading is intended to describe is visible and not hidden from assistive technologies.
   Otherwise, cases such as expandable content using a heading might fail this rule while Success
   Criterion 2.4.6 Headings and Labels is still satisfied." A heading with `firstContent: null`
   (the section is a widget or an image) is judged from the screenshot. Record `cannot-tell` for a
   heading over content the evaluator cannot read. Done when every applicable heading is marked
   "describes", "does not describe" or `cannot-tell` on its findings line.

3. Inventory the programmatic labels of form fields:

   ```bash
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/forms.js
   agent-browser eval --stdin <<'EOF'
   (() => {
     const ROLES = ['checkbox', 'combobox', 'listbox', 'menuitemcheckbox', 'menuitemradio', 'radio',
       'searchbox', 'slider', 'spinbutton', 'switch', 'textbox'];
     const TYPES = ['color', 'date', 'datetime-local', 'file', 'month', 'password', 'time', 'week'];
     const css = (e) => {
       const p = [];
       let n = e;
       while (n && n.nodeType === 1 && n !== document.documentElement) {
         if (n.id) { p.unshift('#' + CSS.escape(n.id)); break; }
         const t = n.tagName.toLowerCase();
         const sib = n.parentElement
           ? [...n.parentElement.children].filter((c) => c.tagName === n.tagName)
           : [];
         p.unshift(sib.length > 1 ? t + ':nth-of-type(' + (sib.indexOf(n) + 1) + ')' : t);
         n = n.parentElement;
       }
       return p.join(' > ');
     };
     const roleAttr = (e) => (e.getAttribute('role') || '').trim().split(/\s+/)[0].toLowerCase();
     const visible = (e) => {
       const s = getComputedStyle(e);
       if (s.visibility !== 'visible' || Number(s.opacity) === 0) return false;
       for (let n = e; n; n = n.parentElement) {
         if (getComputedStyle(n).display === 'none') return false;
       }
       const r = e.getBoundingClientRect();
       if (r.width === 0 || r.height === 0) return false;
       return r.bottom + window.scrollY > 0 && r.right + window.scrollX > 0;
     };
     const implicit = (e) => {
       const t = e.tagName;
       if (t === 'TEXTAREA') return 'textbox';
       if (t === 'SELECT') return e.multiple || e.size > 1 ? 'listbox' : 'combobox';
       if (t !== 'INPUT') return '';
       const ty = (e.getAttribute('type') || 'text').toLowerCase();
       if (['checkbox', 'radio'].includes(ty)) return ty;
       if (ty === 'range') return 'slider';
       if (ty === 'number') return 'spinbutton';
       if (ty === 'search') return e.hasAttribute('list') ? 'combobox' : 'searchbox';
       if (['text', 'tel', 'url', 'email'].includes(ty)) {
         return e.hasAttribute('list') ? 'combobox' : 'textbox';
       }
       return '';
     };
     const headingBefore = (e) => {
       const FOLLOWING = Node.DOCUMENT_POSITION_FOLLOWING;
       const hs = [...document.querySelectorAll('h1,h2,h3,h4,h5,h6,[role="heading"]')]
         .filter((h) => visible(h) && (h.compareDocumentPosition(e) & FOLLOWING));
       const h = hs[hs.length - 1];
       return h ? h.textContent.replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim().slice(0, 80) : null;
     };
     const out = [];
     for (const e of document.querySelectorAll('input,select,textarea,[role]')) {
       const r = roleAttr(e);
       const im = implicit(e);
       const role = ROLES.includes(r) ? r : (!r && ROLES.includes(im) ? im : null);
       const type = (e.getAttribute('type') || '').toLowerCase();
       const byType = e.tagName === 'INPUT' && !r && !im && TYPES.includes(type);
       if (!role && !byType) continue;
       const labels = [];
       if (e.labels) for (const l of e.labels) labels.push({ kind: 'label', el: l });
       const ids = (e.getAttribute('aria-labelledby') || '').trim().split(/\s+/);
       for (const id of ids.filter(Boolean)) {
         const n = document.getElementById(id);
         if (n && !labels.some(x => x.el === n)) labels.push({ kind: 'aria-labelledby', el: n });
       }
       const fs = e.closest('fieldset');
       const lg = fs && fs.querySelector('legend');
       out.push({
         selector: css(e),
         role: role || ('input[type=' + type + ']'),
         controlVisible: visible(e),
         applicable: visible(e) && labels.some(l => visible(l.el)),
         labels: labels.map(l => ({
           kind: l.kind,
           selector: css(l.el),
           text: l.el.textContent.replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim().slice(0, 80),
           visible: visible(l.el),
           ariaHidden: l.el.closest('[aria-hidden="true"]') !== null,
         })),
         legend: lg && visible(lg) ? lg.textContent.replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim().slice(0, 80) : null,
         headingBefore: headingBefore(e),
         placeholder: e.getAttribute('placeholder'),
       });
     }
     return out;
   })()
   EOF
   ```

   Each row is one control cc0f0a applies to, its `role` naming which half of the applicability
   caught it (a semantic role from the list, or `input[type=…]` for the type list). `applicable:
true` means both conditions hold — the control is visible and at least one programmatic label is
   visible — and every `labels` entry with `visible: true` is a separate test target for step 4.
   `ariaHidden: true` with `visible: true` stays a target: "The programmatic label does not need to
   be included in the accessibility tree for this rule to apply" (Passed Example 4), and hiding a
   visible label from assistive technology "is a failure of Success Criterion 4.1.2 [...] but not of
   Success Criterion 2.4.6" (its Assumption). `legend` and `headingBefore` are the visible context
   step 4 reads with the label; `headingBefore` is the nearest visible heading-looking element in
   reading order and counts as visual context whether or not it is a semantic heading. A label with
   `visible: false` is not a target (Inapplicable Example 2), and `controlVisible: false` drops the
   whole row (Inapplicable Example 3).

   Two cases the rule leaves and this criterion keeps, both read from `forms.js`: a row with
   `applicable: false` and a `placeholder` has that placeholder as the only presented label — judge
   it in step 4; a `nameSource` of `'aria-label'`, `'title'` or `'none'` with nothing visible is no
   label at all — hand the absence to 3.3.2 and the name to 4.1.2. A `label[for]` pointing at
   something that is not a form control is a programmatic label of nothing (Inapplicable Example 1):
   note it for 1.3.1 and move on. Done when every visible field has its list of visible labels, or
   its placeholder, or a note handing it to 3.3.2.

4. Judge each visible programmatic label (cc0f0a Expectation: "Each test target, together with its
   visual context, describes the purpose of the associated element"). Read it with `legend`,
   `headingBefore`, the field's own `describedbyText` and the screenshot from step 2; the rule's
   other assumption is that "the programmatic labels of an element are also part of its visual
   context", so two labels on one field are read together (Passed Example 6: `Shipping` and `Name`
   on one `aria-labelledby`, each descriptive within the context the other gives).

   Yes when the label names what to enter or choose: "First name" (Passed Examples 1, 2 and 3, as a
   `label` wrapping, a `label for` and an `aria-labelledby` target), "Date of birth" on a
   `type="date"` field (Passed Example 7). Yes also when a repeated label is told apart by a visible
   heading: "Name" and "Street" under a visible `<h2>Shipping</h2>` and a visible `<h2>Billing</h2>`
   pass, because "the headings provide a visual context that differentiates the purpose of the
   otherwise identically named form fields" (Passed Example 5).

   No when the label names something else: "Menu" on a first-name field (Failed Examples 1, 2 and 3,
   the same defect in all three association styles), "Info" on a `type="date"` field (Failed Example
   6). No when identical labels sit on fields with different purposes and the heading that would
   tell them apart is not visible — `<h2 style="position: absolute; top: -9999px">Shipping
address</h2>` over "Name:" and "Street:" fails because "The headings are not visible. Therefore,
   they do not provide visual context" (Failed Example 4); the step 3 rows show this as an
   `offScreen` heading in step 1 with `headingBefore: null` or a different heading here. No when the
   only visible label is generic while the descriptive one is hidden: `<span id="search"
style="display: none">Search</span>` with `<button id="submit">Go</button>` both labelling the
   field leaves "Go", which "has no visual context, and is not descriptive" (Failed Example 5).

   A placeholder as the only label reading "Enter here" or "Input" describes nothing; "Name" on a
   field whose `value` shows a postcode names the wrong thing. Done when every visible label and
   every placeholder-only label is marked "describes" or "does not describe" on its findings line.

5. Judge the labels of other components. Neither ACT rule covers them, so the verdict is the
   evaluator's reading and the certainty comes from the "Deciding" list:

   ```bash
   agent-browser snapshot -i
   agent-browser eval --stdin < <skill-dir>/scripts/probes/images.js
   ```

   `snapshot -i` lists every button, tab, menu item and switch with its name; `images.js` entries
   with `inButton: true` and an empty `controlText` are icon-only labels, `name` being the icon's
   text alternative and `nearbyText` its context. The visible text, or the icon in context, names
   the action or the choice: "Search", "Add to basket", "Next"; the Understanding's own example is
   "A search text input [...] followed by a button containing a magnifying glass icon that activates
   the search function. The icon has the string "search" as programmatically determinable label."
   Judge an icon by the context it sits in, because "the same image can be interpreted differently
   in different contexts. However, it can still be considered descriptive if its use is commonly
   understood in each context": a loupe "when accompanying a text field [...] is commonly
   interpreted as indicating the field is for entering and submitting a search query", while
   "placed on or near another image, a loupe or magnifying glass icon is commonly interpreted as a
   means to view a magnified version of the image". An icon named for the wrong one of those two
   conventions fails here even though its text alternative exists.

   It does not describe when it reads "Button", "Click here" or "Submit" beside nothing that says
   what is submitted, when a tab strip reads "Tab 1, Tab 2, Tab 3", or when an icon's name is its
   file name or "icon" (that alternative goes to 1.1.1). Opening a new window unannounced is
   advisory only ([G201](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G201),
   [SCR24](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/client-side-script/SCR24)): note it, never fail
   it. Link text is judged under 2.4.4. Done when every labelled control has a verdict on its
   findings line.

6. Repeat steps 1 to 5 in each recorded state: once it is reached, rerun `structure.js`, `forms.js`
   and both scripts above, and judge what appeared — a dialog's heading against its content, a
   wizard step's heading against its fields, an error summary's heading against the errors under it,
   the labels of fields that only exist after a choice. A state that expands collapsed content is
   also where step 2's second assumption is settled: judge the heading against the expanded content,
   not the collapsed placeholder. Done when every state has its own rows in the findings file.

7. Compare with the samples already evaluated, so that one template defect becomes one issue:

   ```bash
   grep -h '^2\.4\.6 |' .notes/audit/findings/*--structure.md | sort -u
   ```

   The same non-describing heading or label text on several samples is one defect of the template or
   component that generates it; list the other samples on that issue. The same _describing_ text
   repeated is not a defect: identical section titles across articles are an editorial convention
   the Understanding passes. Done when every "does not describe" line is either grouped with its
   duplicates on other samples or marked unique to this one.

What the accessibility tree cannot show: whether a screen reader's heading list is the way this
audience actually navigates, whether an abbreviation is house language the readers know, and
whether an icon convention is understood by the site's audience. None of those is settled by a
snapshot — take them to the confirmation queue with the exact text and the screenshot.

## Deciding

- Fail (certainty ≥ 90): a heading in the tree whose text is a placeholder or template token
  ("Heading", "Lorem ipsum", "Untitled", "{{ title }}", "Insert heading here") (90); a visible
  `label` element or `aria-labelledby` text that is a placeholder token ("Label", "{{ label }}",
  "Enter here") (90).
- Fail (70–89): a heading naming a topic other than the content that follows it, confirmed against
  the whole section and not only `firstContent` (b49b2e Failed Example 1) (85); a label naming
  something other than what the field collects (cc0f0a Failed Examples 1, 2, 3 and 6) (85); a
  visible programmatic label that is `aria-hidden` and names the wrong thing — still this criterion,
  because visibility and not the tree decides applicability (85); identical labels on fields with
  different purposes and no visible legend, heading or text to tell them apart (cc0f0a Failed
  Example 4) (80); a generic visible label with the descriptive text hidden (cc0f0a Failed Example 5) (80);
  a placeholder as the only label that does not name the data ("Enter here", "Type here")
  (80); numbered or ordinal headings over content with a nameable topic ("Section 1", "Step 2"
  without the step's subject) (75); a button, tab or menu item labelled "Button", "Click here",
  "Tab 1" or "Item" (75); an icon label carrying the convention of the wrong context — a loupe that
  magnifies an image but is named "Search", or one on a text field named "Zoom" (70); several
  identical headings at one level over different content within one page ("More", "Details") with
  nothing else in the heading list to tell them apart (70); a visually styled heading without markup
  whose text does not describe its section (70; the markup goes to 1.3.1).
- Needs confirmation (50–69): a heading that describes its section but not the first perceivable
  content after it, where CSS positioning moved that content (b49b2e Assumption 1) (55): ask whether
  the rendered order matches what a screen reader reads; a heading whose content is hidden from
  assistive technology until expanded and which describes the expanded content (b49b2e Assumption 2)
  (50): record the expanded state's verdict and ask only if the section cannot be expanded; a
  one-word heading or label that is an abbreviation or domain term ("KvK", "IBAN", "SKU") (60): ask
  whether the audience knows it; a marketing or question heading over a sign-up or call-to-action
  block ("Ready?", "Let's go") (55): ask whether the site's readers take it as the section's
  purpose; an icon-only label with an uncommon convention (a funnel for filter, a bell for
  notifications) (55): ask whether the audience recognises it; an `h1`–`h6` with
  `role="presentation"` that the snapshot still shows as a heading (Presentational Roles Conflict
  Resolution) (50): ask which baseline the audit assumes; a heading or label over placeholder
  content on a staging site (50): ask for the live content.
- Pass: every heading (in the tree or styled) names the topic or purpose of the content it
  introduces, and every visible label, read with its legend, heading and surrounding text, names
  what the field collects or the control does, in every recorded state; a word or an icon counts.
  Passing both ACT rules is not enough on its own — their outcome mapping is "All passed outcomes:
  success criterion needs further testing" — so a `pass` also needs step 5's buttons, tabs and icon
  labels and step 1's styled headings judged.
- Not present: no heading (in the tree or styled), no visible field label and no labelled control
  in the sample or any recorded state.
- False positives to avoid: a missing label (3.3.2) or name (4.1.2); a visible label the name leaves
  out (2.5.3); a visible label hidden from assistive technology, which is 4.1.2 and not this
  criterion (cc0f0a Assumption); a skipped level, a heading not marked up or hidden from the tree
  (1.3.1); an empty heading (a 1.3.1 note, no ACT outcome); a short heading or label — "A word, or
  even a single character, may suffice" (Understanding) and "may be sufficient" (b49b2e); a heading
  whose second and later paragraphs are about something else, since only the first perceivable
  content is judged (b49b2e Passed Example 6); the same section titles repeated across articles by
  an editorial convention — the Understanding passes "Summary, Introduction, [other sections unique
  to this article], Conclusion, Author Biography, Glossary, and Bibliography" on every paper of a
  conference site, "creating a useful balance between the uniqueness of the articles and the
  consistency of the section headings"; a repeated label that a visible legend or heading
  disambiguates (cc0f0a Passed Example 5); a magnifying glass named "Search" beside a search field;
  an `img` heading with a describing `alt` (b49b2e Passed Example 3); an off-screen heading in the
  tree (b49b2e Passed Example 5); an orphan `label[for]` pointing at a non-control (cc0f0a
  Inapplicable Example 1, a 1.3.1 note); a heading worded unlike the page title or the link that led
  here.

## Reporting

- File under: 2.4.6. A missing label goes to 3.3.2; a missing name to 4.1.2; a visible label that
  the name leaves out to 2.5.3; a visible label hidden from assistive technology to 4.1.2; wrong
  markup, a wrong level, an empty heading or a heading kept out of the tree to 1.3.1; vague link
  text to 2.4.4; an icon's non-describing text alternative to 1.1.1; a vague page title to 2.4.2.
  One issue per wording defect: every placeholder heading from one template is one issue naming each
  sample it is on, identical labels inside one form are one issue, and a defect repeated on other
  samples is one issue that names them.
- Title patterns: "Section headings do not say what the sections contain", "Form labels do not say
  what to enter", "Repeated 'Name' labels cannot be told apart", "Heading above the opening hours
  says 'Weather'", "Buttons are labelled 'Go' instead of the action they carry out"
- Severity: High when the labels of a form on a task path (login, checkout, an application form) do
  not say what to enter, or a page's headings are all generic so the heading list gives no way in on
  a long page; Medium when one section or field in a task is mislabelled while the rest is clear, or
  the defect comes from a template used site-wide; Low when the legend, text or value next to a
  vague heading or label settles the meaning.
- Type: Content by default, the wording is editorial. Technical when a template or component
  generates the text (a `{{ title }}` token, a CMS block repeating "Read more" as a heading, a
  wrong `aria-labelledby` target). Design when an icon-only label relies on an uncommon icon.
- Evidence: a full-page screenshot with the subject drawn on it, so the heading or label and the
  content it belongs to are in one image:

  ```bash
  agent-browser highlight "<selector from step 1 or 3>"
  agent-browser screenshot .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--2.4.6-<n>.png --full
  agent-browser screenshot "<fieldset selector>" .notes/audit/evidence/<sample>--2.4.6-form.png
  ```

  Crop to the heading plus the first lines it introduces, or to the field plus its legend; for a
  form the element screenshot of the `fieldset` shows the repeated labels side by side. The body
  quotes the heading or label and the first line of what it introduces. No GIF.

- Recommendation: reword each heading so that it names the topic of its section, key words first
  ([G130](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G130), whose Example 1 puts the
  distinguishing word at the front); reword each label so that it names the data asked for or the
  action taken ([G131](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G131)); where fields repeat,
  add a visible legend or heading that tells the groups apart. Minimal examples:

  ```html
  <h1>Disaster preparation</h1>
  <h2>Flood preparation</h2>
  <!-- not "Preparation for floods": key word first -->

  <h2>Opening hours</h2>
  <!-- not <h2>Info</h2> -->
  <label for="fname">First name</label>
  <!-- not <label>Enter here</label> -->
  <input id="fname" type="text" name="fname" />

  <h2>Shipping</h2>
  <!-- visible, so it is visual context -->
  <label>Name <input type="text" name="shipping-name" /></label>

  <button>Search</button>
  <!-- not <button>Go</button> -->
  <button><img src="loupe.svg" alt="Search" /></button>
  ```

  Where the wording comes from a template or a component, fix it at the source and name the
  component in the issue, so the other samples are covered by the same change.

- Related: 1.3.1 (the same heading or label must also be marked up; the Understanding says each can
  pass while the other fails, and the Techniques note adds that "Headings and labels must be
  programmatically determined, per Success Criterion 1.3.1"); 3.3.2 (a label must exist); 4.1.2 (the
  accessible name, including a visible label hidden from assistive technology); 2.5.3 (the visible
  label inside the name); 2.4.4 (link text); 2.4.2 (the page title); 2.4.10 at AAA (sections need
  headings at all).
